Every feature

Everything Slate does, area by area, down to each setting and shortcut. Almost all of it is set from the Settings app, and every setting is searchable there.

 Slate is moving quickly, so this list may be a step behind the latest version.

 Session

Its own session

  • Installs as a Slate entry on any login screen; your existing desktop stays as it is
  • Runs Hyprland with Slate's own config, separate from ~/.config/hypr
  • Starts on a plain dark background that matches the login screen, without Hyprland's or your distro's default wallpaper flashing up first
  • Your own tweaks go in ~/.config/slate/hypr/ and are never overwritten
  • Slate restarts itself on its own code changes; Restart the shell in Settings → About
  • A welcome on first start: tiling or floating, colours from the wallpaper, theming your other apps, where the bar and dock go, and the shortcuts, each with a recommendation

Password prompts

  • A built-in authentication agent, so admin prompts (pkexec, system settings) appear in Slate's style

 Menu bar

Placement

  • Top, bottom, left or right of the screen
  • Auto-hide, revealed at the screen edge
  • Flares into the screen with rounded corners
  • Dimmed on monitors you aren't using
  • Hover descriptions: point at anything for what it does

Left side

  • Control centre button (the slate mark); right-click it for Settings, the wallpaper, Do Not Disturb, Night Light, glass, updates, restarting Slate, locking and logging out
  • Apps button, with right-click shortcuts to Settings pages
  • Spaces button: save and bring back sets of workspaces (see Windows)
  • Workspaces as dots, with a springy indicator
  • The focused window's icon and title (the icon can be turned off)

Right side

  • App tray icons, with menus styled like the dock's; optionally tucked behind one button
  • Notifications, Wi-Fi, keyboard layout, Bluetooth, volume and battery, each optional; optionally tucked behind one button
  • Clipboard (with how many items it holds) and screenshot buttons, off until you switch them on
  • Game mode button (off until you switch it on): its panel turns off glass, blur, see-through windows, shadows, animations, live window previews (the switcher and overview show each window's shape and app icon instead), notification pop-ups (they still go to the list) and ignoring the touchpad while you type, for smoother games, and puts them all back as you had them after
  • Optional values beside them: clipboard items, network name, layout code, connected devices, volume and battery percentages
  • Clock: 12 or 24 hour, seconds, the date
  • Power button

 Panels and the control centre

Control centre

  • Your profile: name, picture over the wallpaper, user and host, uptime; click the picture to change it, in a picker of its own with your picture folders, a thumbnail grid and a round preview
  • Media controls for whatever's playing
  • The time and date with today's weather and a four-day forecast
  • Quick toggles: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Caffeine (stay awake), Night light, Do not disturb, Performance mode
  • Shortcuts to Settings and the power menu

Panels from the bar

  • Wi-Fi: networks to join, on/off
  • Bluetooth: devices to connect, on/off
  • Keyboard layout: switch between your layouts
  • Sound: volume and output
  • Battery: charge and power mode (Saver, Balanced, Performance)
  • Calendar: a month view with the forecast
  • Notifications: the history
  • Clipboard: the latest things you copied, to copy again, or clear
  • Screenshot: an area, a window or the screen, and the screenshots folder
  • Power: lock, log out, restart, shut down; anything that ends the session asks first
  • Restarting can reopen your apps afterwards: a switch on the restart dialog (on by default) brings every window back on the same workspace, in the same place, then returns you to the workspace you were on

 Dock

Apps

  • Pinned and running apps, with a dot under running ones
  • Magnifies under the pointer: icon size, magnification and spread are adjustable
  • Icons bounce while their app opens
  • Bottom, top, left or right; auto-hides when a window needs the room
  • Its corners follow your icon shape

Right-click menus

  • The app's open windows, to jump to
  • The app's own actions (a new private window, and so on)
  • New Window, Pin to Dock / Unpin from Dock
  • Close Window, or Close 2 Windows
  • Force Quit, for an app that has stopped responding
  • Centred on the icon, with a pointer to it

 Launcher

Find anything

  • Tap Super on its own, or Super+Space, and type
  • Apps sorted by how often you use them (optional)
  • Every setting in Settings is searchable from here
  • Does sums as you type
  • Shrinks smoothly to fit the results as you type, with the search box staying put
  • Open an app on a new workspace at the end, or to the left or the right, with a key

Layout

  • A list, with or without descriptions
  • Or a grid of icons: columns and rows are adjustable, icons scale to fit, and the name shows at the bottom

 Windows

Tiling or traditional

  • Windows tile themselves by default
  • Or floating windows with title bars: drag to move, close and maximise buttons, double-click to maximise and click again to restore
  • Float or tile any window with a key
  • Maximise, full screen, split the other way, resize with the arrow keys
  • Move and resize with Super and the mouse

Look and feel

  • Window borders, spacing between windows, and space around a lone window
  • Dim inactive windows (Settings itself never dims, so you can watch a change take effect)
  • Focus follows the pointer (optional)
  • Picture-in-picture videos float and stay on top
  • Games (Steam and gamescope) go full screen on their own workspace
  • Terminals step aside for the apps they open

Switcher

  • Super+Tab: every window in a circular strip, with live previews and app icons on them (both optional)
  • Alt+Tab for the next window

Spaces

  • Save the apps on one or more workspaces, and where their windows sit, as a space, from the Spaces button on the bar
  • Pick the workspaces from small live previews of them, then give the space a name, an icon and a colour (pastels, or any colour of your own)
  • Click a space to bring it back: each workspace opens as a new one, its apps start, and their windows go back to the same tiles, floating spots and sizes
  • Windows opened from a space get a thin top border in its colour and wear its icon in their top left corner; the badge fades across to the other top corner when the pointer comes near, the window's icon on the menu bar sits on the space's colour, and the badge shows on the window in the window switcher and the workspace overview (each can be switched off in Settings → Spaces)
  • Saving under a name you already use replaces that space; delete one from its row (it asks first, in the middle of the screen)
  • Settings → Spaces: the button on the menu bar, and each way their windows are marked
  • slate ipc call spaces restore <name> brings one back from a key binding or script

 Workspaces

Overview

  • Super+Down: every workspace as a large live card, optionally with each window's app icon on it
  • Jump to one, or hold Shift to lift a card and move it along
  • Card size is adjustable

Moving around

  • Next and previous workspace, with an on-screen indicator, wrapping round from the last to the first (optional; off, the overview's row ends there too)
  • Go to, or move a window to, workspace 1–9 (per monitor too)
  • Move a window to a new workspace after the last one in use
  • Always keep an empty workspace, last in the row (optional)
  • A secret workspace, to show or hide over the others
  • Follows apps you're sent to: a link opened from another app takes you to the browser

 Notifications and indicators

Notifications

  • Slate's own notification server
  • Pop-ups: position, how long they stay, and how many at once
  • A history to catch up on, and clear
  • Do not disturb
  • A soft chime when one pops up (optional)
  • Shown on your main display

On-screen indicators

  • Volume, microphone, brightness and keyboard backlight
  • The workspace dots when you switch

 Desktop

Wallpaper

  • Pick from a folder of your own, shown as thumbnails in Settings
  • Crossfades when it changes
  • Changes by itself on a timer or at each login, in a shuffled order (optional)
  • Leaves out small pictures (icons, avatars), with a minimum size

Right-click menu

  • App launcher, Terminal, Files
  • Change wallpaper, Next wallpaper
  • Display settings, Settings

Hot corners

  • Each corner can lock the screen, open the launcher, the workspace overview, the control centre or notifications, or turn the screen off
  • An adjustable delay; disarmed while locked
  • Set on a picture of the screen in Settings, showing what each corner does

Night light

  • A warmer screen in the evening, with adjustable warmth
  • On by hand, sunset to sunrise for your location, or a custom schedule

 Appearance

Theme and colour

  • Dark, light, or automatic by sunset (light mode is still being polished, so Slate asks before switching to it)
  • Accent colour from a preset, any colour you like, or the wallpaper (optional), with an Accent boldness slider for how vivid the wallpaper's colour is
  • Colourful (the default: soft pastels, such as each Settings page in its own colour, which settings found in the launcher and Slate's notifications share) or Monotone (the accent and greys)
  • Buttons, rows and menu items light up in the accent colour as you point at them
  • Presets that set the theme, colours, glass and folder colours (from the wallpaper) in one go (asks first; your wallpaper stays): Slate Default (back to how Slate starts out), Liquid Glass and Metro Flat
  • Background tint from the wallpaper (optional), with a Tint boldness slider for how much colour it adds
  • Folder colour for file managers (Papirus): from the wallpaper, the theme's own, or any Papirus colour
  • Everything (bar, panels, Settings, themed apps) shifts together when the wallpaper changes

Style

  • Rounded screen corners
  • Workspace indicators as circles or squares
  • App icons as tiles, squircles, circles, squares or their original shape, filled or not, optionally tinted with the accent; tiles in the theme's colour or a deep shade of each icon's own

Fonts, size and motion

  • Interface font (Geist by default) and code font (Geist Mono by default)
  • Interface size, 75–150%: sizes Slate itself on every display, on top of each display's scale, leaving apps as they are (asks before restarting Slate to apply it)
  • Glass: the bar, dock, panels, menus, Settings and the lock and login screens turn see-through with the desktop blurred behind, with opacity, blur, shine and gloss to adjust, and window borders get a sheen across their top-left corner (off by default)
  • Fast blur (behind see-through windows and glass, the wallpaper only, for smooth animation; on by default) and a blur quality setting (Medium by default)
  • Animations: normal, faster or off, for windows and workspaces and for Slate's own panels, fades and highlights alike
  • Workspace switching: Slide Fade, Slide, Crossfade, vertical slides or none

 App theming

Opt-in, app by app, in Settings → App theming. Each app you switch on follows Slate's colours, light or dark, and the accent, with text colours kept readable against the background (in light mode too). Slate backs up a config before it first changes it, and switching an app off puts it back the way it was, keeping your own edits.

  • Qt apps (qt6ct)

 Displays

Monitors

  • Arrange them by dragging
  • Turn each on or off; resolution, refresh rate, scale and rotation per monitor
  • Choose the main display, where notifications appear
  • Changes revert by themselves unless you keep them

 Keyboard, mouse and touchpad

Keyboard

  • Several layouts, with a shortcut to switch between them and a menu bar item
  • Caps Lock as itself, another Ctrl, Escape, nothing, or the layout switcher
  • Repeat delay and speed (500 ms and 16 a second to start with)

Mouse

  • Pointer speed (for mice and touchpads) and acceleration
  • Natural scrolling
  • Pointer size, with Slate's own cursor

Touchpad

  • Scroll speed
  • Tap to click
  • Ignore while typing

 Sound

Output and input

  • Choose the speakers or headphones, volume, mute
  • Choose the microphone, input level, mute

Sound effects

  • Each with its own volume slider, from off up
  • A tick when you change the volume
  • A soft chime with notifications
  • A gentle, questioning sound for confirmations, password prompts and urgent notifications
  • A barely-there tap on buttons and menus
  • A soft swish switching workspaces and windows, drifting the way you go
  • A gentle welcome when you log in, as Slate fades in from the dark, and a closing sound as it fades to black when you log out, restart or shut down

 Power

Lid and power button

  • Closing the lid: suspend, lock, turn the screen off, or nothing
  • Power button: show the power menu, suspend, shut down (asks first), lock, or nothing

Power mode

  • Saver, Balanced or Performance, chosen separately for on battery and plugged in

Restarting

  • Reopen apps after restarting: each window comes back on the same workspace and screen, tiled or floating just as it was (also a switch on the restart dialog)

 Lock and login

Lock screen

  • Your wallpaper and picture, and your account password
  • A countdown after too many tries
  • A power menu on the lock screen (optional)
  • Lock before sleep

When you're away

  • Lock, turn the screen off, and suspend after times you choose
  • Waits while media plays

Screensaver

  • A clock on your wallpaper (dimmed and slowly drifting), a slideshow of your wallpaper folder, or a clock on black that moves so nothing burns in
  • Starts after a time you choose, before the lock; any key, click or pointer move puts it away
  • Lock on wake (optional): waking it brings up the lock screen
  • Settings → Screensaver, with a preview

Login screen

  • A matching greetd login screen, with your wallpaper and picture
  • Follows your wallpaper, theme and picture automatically; a new design asks for your password once, with an urgent notification that stays until you answer it
  • Resync from Settings → Debug, if you changed something outside Slate
  • Safe updates: each one is test-loaded before it's installed, and if the login screen ever fails at boot, the last version that worked takes over, then a basic emergency login

 Apps, startup and time

Default apps

  • Terminal, web browser, files, text editor and calculator, used by the shortcuts and menus

Startup apps

  • Apps to open at each login
  • After a restart that reopens your apps, each opens on a workspace of its own, so they don't crowd the reopened ones

Date, time and location

  • Time zone, and setting the time automatically
  • Your location, for the weather and sunset times: places come up as you type, to pick from a list

 Built-in tools

Screenshots

  • An area or a window (the screen freezes while you pick), or the whole screen
  • Saved to your chosen folder with your chosen names, and copied
  • Then optionally opened in swash to mark up; saving there updates the same file

Clipboard, emoji and colours

  • Clipboard history, text and images (Super+V), sized to what's in it; passwords are never kept, and history can be switched off
  • An emoji picker (Super+.)
  • Pick a colour from anywhere on screen (Super+P), copied
  • Zoom in and out of the screen

 The Settings app

Finding things

  • Opens on All Settings: a tile for every page, with a search across all settings; every other page has the same search at its top
  • A Debug page (from All Settings): resync the login screen, set how much detail goes in Slate's log (asks before restarting Slate), export recent logs with system details to attach to a bug report (your location is left out), and factory reset Slate: it asks twice, can clear your own Hyprland tweaks and QML too, and keeps everything in a backup folder first
  • Pages: General, Appearance, Displays, Keyboard and Mouse, Sound, Power, Windows, Workspaces, Notifications, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Menu Bar, Dock, Launcher, Spaces, Hot Corners, Lock and Idle, Shortcuts, Default Apps, Startup Apps, Date and Time, Screenshots, App Theming, Updates, Debug, About
  • Restore defaults on each page
  • Comes back on the same page, scrolled to the same place, if Slate reloads or restarts while it's open

Updates

  • Checks for a new version daily, with a notification when one's out
  • What's new, and updating in one click, keeping your settings and tweaks
  • Keeps the version before, so you can go back to it

Shortcuts page

  • Every keyboard shortcut, searchable, including ones you add to your own Hyprland config
  • Three sets to choose from: Slate's, Hyprland's defaults, or Hyprland's with Slate's added where the keys are free

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth

  • Networks to join, with passwords
  • Devices to connect, and finding new ones

 Keyboard shortcuts

Slate's set, the default. All of them are listed, and searchable, in Settings → Shortcuts, where you can switch to Hyprland's default shortcuts, or Hyprland's with Slate's added on the keys they leave free.

 Under the hood

Built on

  • Hyprland 0.57 or newer, with its Lua config
  • Quickshell 0.3 or newer (QML)
  • Geist and Tabler icons, bundled

Scriptable

  • Everything is reachable from a terminal: slate ipc call settings page displays, … launcher open, … wallpaper set <file> and more
  • Settings live in one JSON file
  • Your own tweaks in ~/.config/slate: Hyprland files for environment, apps, monitors, shortcuts, window rules, startup commands and anything else, and a QML file loaded into Slate, each made with instructions in it, kept on updates, and reloaded as you save (mistakes show in a notification)
  • Slate reloads as its files change; if a change has a mistake, a notification says what and where, Slate keeps running as it was, and reloads as soon as you save the fix

Installs on

  • Arch, Debian and Ubuntu, Fedora, openSUSE and NixOS, with one installer
  • Open source under the MIT licence
