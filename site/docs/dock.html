The desktop

The dock

The dock holds the apps you use most and the ones you have open, with what's playing, your downloads and the waste basket close at hand. It sits along the bottom of your screen to start with.

What's on it

From start to end:

  • The Apps button, which opens the launcher.
  • What's playing, while something is.
  • Your pinned apps, then the other apps you have open. Running apps have a dot beneath them.
  • Downloads and the waste basket at the end.

Thin dividers keep these groups apart. You can switch the dividers, the Apps button, what's playing, Downloads and the waste basket off in  Settings → Dock.

Using your apps

Click an app to open it; its icon bounces while it starts. Click an app that's already open to go to its window, and click again to step through its other windows.

Window previews

Rest the pointer on an app with windows open and live previews of them appear just above the dock. Click one to go to it. You can turn previews off, or choose how long to rest first, from Instant up to 3 seconds. In game mode they show as titles only.

Badges and progress

An app can show a count on its icon: the unread number it publishes itself, or the notifications it's sent since you last went to it. While an app downloads or copies something, a progress bar shows how far it's got. Badges stay put when Slate restarts.

Hover effects

Icons respond as the pointer moves along the dock. Choose how in  Settings → Dock → Icons:

EffectWhat it does
MagnifyThe icons near the pointer grow, the one under it most. This is the default.
TiltThe icons turn in 3D towards the pointer, and the ones near it straighten up to face you.
Magnify and tiltBoth at once.
FadeThe icons away from the pointer fade, so the ones near it stand out.
Fade and magnifyThe icons near the pointer grow, and the ones away from it fade.
GlowThe icon under the pointer gets a ring and a soft glow, in its own colour.
Glow and magnifyThe icons near the pointer grow, and the one under it glows.
RaiseJust the icon under the pointer lifts up a little.
NoneThe icons stay as they are.

Icon size, Magnification (how large the icon under the pointer grows) and Magnification spread (how many neighbours join in) are on the same page.

When the dock is full

When the dock gets too long for your screen, Slate makes the icons smaller, but never smaller than 24 pixels, so they're still easy to click. If it still doesn't fit:

  • What's playing becomes a single icon.
  • Then the apps you haven't pinned go into More, the three dots near the end of the dock, the ones you opened longest ago first. Click More to see them in a grid, as Downloads shows its files. Click an app there to go to it, or right-click it for its menu.
  • Your pinned apps stay on the dock. Only if there's no room even for them do they go into More too.

As room comes back (you close apps, or move the dock to a longer edge), they come back to the dock. Badges still count: More shows the total for the apps in it.

Pinning and unpinning

  • To pin an app, drag it from among your open apps into the pinned ones, or right-click it and choose Pin to Dock.
  • To reorder, drag an icon along the dock. The others part to make room for it.
  • To unpin, pull a pinned app well up off the dock. A warning appears above it before you let go, so nothing goes by accident. If the app is open, it stays on the dock with the other running apps until you close it. Unpin from Dock in its right-click menu does the same.

Dropping files

Drag files onto an app on the dock to open them in it, or onto the waste basket to put them in the bin.

Right-click an app

Right-click an app for its menu, pointing at the icon:

  • Its open windows, to go straight to one. The one you're in has a dot beside it.
  • The app's own actions, like a browser's New Private Window, and New Window.
  • Pin to Dock or Unpin from Dock.
  • Close Window (or Close 2 Windows, and so on), which closes every window the app has open.
  • Force Quit, for an app that has stopped responding.


Force Quit stops the app straight away, so anything it hasn't saved is lost. Try Close Window first.

Every menu on the dock is listed in Right-click menus.

The Apps button

Click it to open the launcher. Right-click it for Settings and a few of its pages: Appearance, Wallpaper, Displays, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

What's playing

While something plays, its album art appears after the Apps button, with Previous, Play/Pause and Next beside it and a progress bar beneath. Click the art to go to the app that's playing. Right-click for the controls in a menu, and to show the player. You can hide the art and keep just the controls with Album art in Settings.

Downloads

Click the Downloads stack for a grid of your newest downloads, four across, with pictures shown as thumbnails. Click one to open it. Right-click the stack to open the Downloads folder itself.

The waste basket

The waste basket at the end of the dock looks full when there's something in it. Click it to open it in your file manager. Right-click and choose Empty Waste Basket… to delete what's in it.



Emptying the waste basket deletes its contents for good, so Slate asks first.

Where it goes

The dock can sit on the bottom, top, left or right of your screen. Choose an edge in  Settings → Dock → Position & behaviour, or from the dock's right-click menu. It slides off its old edge and back in at the new one. If you pick the menu bar's edge, the bar moves to the opposite one.

Auto-hide

With Auto-hide on, the dock steps out of the way while the workspace has tiled windows that need the room. It stays in view over an empty workspace or one with only floating windows. Push the pointer to the dock's edge to bring it back.

The dock's own menu

Right-click an empty part of the dock for quick changes, without opening Settings. Each opens a submenu beside it, with your current choice ticked:

  • Auto-hide, ticked while it's on.
  • Position: Top, Bottom, Left or Right.
  • Hover effect: any of the effects above.
  • Icon size: Small, Medium or Large.
  • Show: the Apps button, what's playing, Downloads and the waste basket.
  • Dock settings, to open its page in Settings.

How it looks

The dock's corners follow your icon shape. With glass on (Appearance), the dock has a few more settings of its own: Blur, Background opacity (a little clearer than the rest of Slate to start with), Tile opacity for the tiles behind its icons and buttons, and Icon opacity.
