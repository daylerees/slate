Your computer

Private Mode and the camera

One switch for when nothing should be listening, watching or recording. Private Mode turns off your microphones, your camera, assistants and screen capture together, and puts everything back as it was when you're done. And whenever an app turns your camera on, Slate lets you know.

Turning Private Mode on

Any of these will do:

  • The Private Mode quick switch in the control centre. If it isn't among your eight, add it in  Settings → Control Centre (Control centre and panels).
  • Type "private" in the launcher and switch it from the results.
  • From a terminal or a script: slate ipc call private toggle (or on or off). See The slate command.

It stays on, even if you restart, until you turn it off.

What it does

While it's onWhat happens
MicrophonesEvery microphone is muted, and kept muted: if anything unmutes one, or you plug a new one in, Slate mutes it again straight away.
The cameraOff for every app, cameras plugged in later included.
AssistantsNova and any other assistants using Slate are off, and they can't turn Private Mode off themselves. Talking to Nova never listens.
ScreenshotsSlate doesn't take any. If you press Print, a notification reminds you Private Mode is on.
Screen sharingSharing and recording your screen through the desktop portal, the way video calls and most recording apps ask for it, is stopped.

Your speakers and Slate's own sounds work as usual. The Sound and Webcam pages in Settings say when Private Mode is on, each with a Turn off button.

When you turn it off, everything comes back as it was. Microphones you'd already muted yourself stay muted; only the ones Private Mode muted come back.

What it can't stop

  • Apps already using the camera or a microphone keep them until they let go. Close the call or the app, and they can't have them back.
  • Apps that capture the screen directly, without the desktop portal (like grim or wf-recorder), aren't stopped.

The camera helper

Apps open your camera directly, so only your system can take it away from them. The first time you turn Private Mode on, Slate explains this and offers to add a small helper to your system. It asks for your password that once, and never again. The helper can only switch the camera off and on, nothing else.

If you'd rather not, choose Cancel and Private Mode stays off. If adding the helper doesn't work out, Private Mode still turns on, with everything except the camera, and a notification says so; turn it off and on again to have another go.



The helper's switch only lasts until your computer restarts, so your camera can never be left off by mistake. If Private Mode is still on, Slate simply turns the camera off again as it starts.

On NixOS

On NixOS, your system is set by your configuration, so Slate can't add the helper itself. Private Mode works there without turning the camera off, and a notification lets you know. To have the camera covered too, add the helper through your NixOS configuration.

Knowing when your camera's on

Whenever an app starts using your camera, a small pop-up like the volume one says Camera on, with the app's name, and Camera off once nothing's using it any more.

Some apps reach the camera through PipeWire, and then the pop-up names PipeWire rather than the app.

The menu bar's camera indicator

For a reminder that stays put, add the camera to your menu bar: choose Edit layout from the bar's right-click menu and drag the camera onto the bar (Arranging it your way). It's an eye, lit in your accent colour while an app is using the camera. Point at it to see which app.

The Webcam page in Settings lists which apps are using the camera too.
