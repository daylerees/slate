Your computer

Accessibility

Settings that make Slate easier to see and use, all in one place:  Settings → Accessibility.

Each one works on top of your other settings without changing them. Switch it off again and everything is as you had it, your look included.

Screen reader

Switch on Screen reader, or press Super + Alt + S from anywhere, and Orca reads out what's on the screen and what you're doing, in your apps and in Slate: the menu bar's items, the dock's apps, the launcher's results, menus, notifications, Settings' switches and buttons (saying whether each is on), and the lock and login screens' password box and buttons. The same keys turn it off.



It needs Orca (the orca package); without it, Settings says so. Orca has settings of its own for its voice and how much it says: press Insert + Space while it's running.

Zoom

Zoom magnifies the whole screen around the pointer. Switch on Zoom, or press Super + Alt + Z to turn it on and off from anywhere. Super + Plus and Super + Minus (or the keypad's + and −) zoom in and out a step at a time, up to 5×, and so does the scroll wheel with Super + Alt held; zooming all the way out turns it off.

SettingWhat it does
Zoom levelHow far in it goes when you turn it on, from 1.25× to 5×. Zooming with the keys changes it too, so it comes back where you left it.
Follow the pointerSmoothly moves the view when the pointer nears its edge; In the middle keeps the pointer centered, the view moving with it.

On-screen keyboard

Switch on On-screen keyboard and keys appear at the bottom of the screen: click or tap them to type into whatever app you're in. Windows make room for it, so what you're typing into isn't hidden. Its ⌄ key puts it away, and Super + Alt + K brings it back. ⇧ once is the next letter in capitals, twice is caps lock; #+= has the symbols.

The lock screen has the same keys behind a keyboard button beside the power button, typing your password. On the login screen that button is always there, for anyone who needs it before logging in.



Typing into apps needs wtype (the wtype package); without it, Settings says so. On the lock and login screens the keys work without it.

Finding the pointer

Lost the pointer? Press Super + Alt + P, or Show me under Locate the pointer, and rings ripple out round it for a moment (with Reduce motion, a still ring). To keep track of it as it moves, switch on Trailing pointer for a short trail of fading dots behind it (off in game mode). A bigger pointer is easier to see too: Pointer size is in Settings → Input, along with Repeat delay and Repeat rate, for how long a key is held before it repeats.

High contrast

Text and icons stand out much more: surfaces go nearer black (or white in light mode), text nearer white (or black), and every surface gets a clear edge, the menu bar's containers, the dock and Settings' cards included. The window you're in has a bright border, and the rest a plainly visible one. Glass is off while it's on. Apps themed in App theming take the same colors.

Text size

Larger and Largest make Slate's text 15% and 30% bigger, everywhere from the menu bar to Settings, without the rest growing: icons and layout stay as they are. (To grow everything, there's Interface size in Settings → Appearance.) GTK apps' text grows to match while App theming themes them, and goes back when either is switched off.

Reduce transparency

Every surface is solid: the menu bar, the dock, panels, menus, the launcher and Settings, and your windows too, with nothing blurred or showing through behind them. It applies whatever Glass is set to: while it's on, Glass in Settings → Appearance is grayed out, with a link back here.

Reduce motion

Nothing on screen slides, grows or moves. Windows open, close and move in place, fading in and out; switching workspaces crossfades; and Slate's own panels and menus appear at once. It applies whatever Animations is set to in Settings → Appearance, which is grayed out while it's on.
