Your computer

Power and battery

How hard your computer works, how kindly your battery's treated, and what happens when you close the lid or press the power button. The battery's on the menu bar, and the rest is in  Settings → Power.

The battery panel

Click the battery on the menu bar for its panel: how charged it is and whether it's charging, then the battery's health and its charging limit, where your laptop reports them. Below that you can switch the power mode, and see how busy your processor, memory and disk are.

On a computer with no battery, the same place on the bar is a power button, with the power mode and those readings.

Power mode

Three modes, in the battery panel and as quick switches in the control centre:

  • Saver makes your battery last longer.
  • Balanced is the everyday choice.
  • Performance gives you everything, where your computer offers it.

On battery and plugged in

Slate can switch modes for you. Under Power mode in Settings, choose one for On battery (used when you unplug) and one for Plugged in (used when you plug in). Leave either as Leave it as it is and Slate won't touch it. You can still change the mode from the control centre at any time.

Charging limit

Keeping a battery full wears it out sooner, most of all on a laptop that spends its days plugged in. Where your laptop supports it, Charging limit under Battery stops charging short of full, so the battery stays healthy for years longer. The control centre has a quick switch for it too.

When you switch the limit off, Slate asks first, since charging to full is worth it mainly when you need every hour away from a socket. You can switch it back on at any time.

Lid and power button

SettingChoices
Closing the lidSuspend, lock the screen, turn the screen off, or do nothing.
Power buttonShow the power menu, suspend, shut down (it asks first), lock the screen, or do nothing.

Both start as System default, which leaves things as your computer already had them.

Reopening your apps after a restart

With Reopen apps after restarting on (it is to start with), restarting your computer brings your apps back afterwards: each window opens again on the same workspace and screen, tiled or floating just as it was, and you're returned to the workspace you were on.

The restart dialog has the same switch, so you can choose each time. Your startup apps still open too, each on a workspace of its own, so they don't crowd the ones coming back.

Staying awake

To keep your computer from locking or going to sleep for a while, say during a presentation, use the Caffeine quick switch. See Caffeine.
