Getting started

Your first login

The first time you log in, Slate opens a Welcome window to set up the few things worth choosing. Everything else works straight away, and every choice can be changed later.

The Welcome window

Each step comes with our recommendation, so if you're not sure, you can simply go with it. You can also Skip at any time: Slate works well just as it is.

  1. How would you like your windows? Tiling (recommended): windows sit side by side and share the screen, never overlapping. Or Floating: windows open where you put them and can overlap, with title bars, like the desktops you may already know.
  2. Colours. Dark, Light or Auto (light by day), and an accent colour either picked from your wallpaper (recommended), changing whenever it does, or one you choose. You can also theme your other apps to match: each app's own settings are backed up first, and switching it off in App Theming puts its original back.
  3. Glass or flat? Metro Flat (recommended): solid panels with crisp edges, a little lighter on your computer. Or Liquid Glass: see-through panels with your desktop softly blurred behind. The window changes as you choose, so you can try both.
  4. The menu bar and dock. Click an edge of the screen for each. Most people enjoy the bar along the top and the dock at the bottom. You can also have the dock hide when windows are open.
  5. Keyboard shortcuts. Slate's own (recommended), built around the Super key; Hyprland's defaults, if that's what your fingers know; or Hyprland's with Slate's added where the keys are free.
  6. Meet Nova. Nova, Slate's assistant, asks an AI agent you already use, such as Claude Code, to change settings, arrange windows and more for you. It's off to start with: Open Assistant settings takes you to  Settings → Assistant, which explains the risks before you switch it on.
  7. You're all set. A few keys to help you find your way, and buttons to open Settings or to start using Slate.

To see the Welcome window again, open  Settings → About → Welcome and choose Show the welcome.

The Super key

Most of Slate's shortcuts use Super: the key with your operating system's logo on it. That's the Windows key on most keyboards, or Command (⌘) on a Mac one.

Tap Super on its own to open the launcher, then type the name of an app, a setting or a file. Holding it with another key does something else, so finishing a shortcut never opens the launcher by mistake.

Keys worth knowing

With Slate's shortcuts, these few will take you a long way:

Every shortcut is in Keyboard shortcuts, and in  Settings → Shortcuts, where you can search them.

Finding your way around

  • The menu bar, along the top to start with, has your workspaces, the window you're in, your status icons and the clock. Click anything on it for a panel. Its mark on the left opens the control centre (Super + X), with your quick switches. See The menu bar.
  • The dock holds your favourite and open apps. Drop a running app among the pinned ones to keep it there. See The dock.
  • The launcher finds apps, settings, open windows and recent files, and does sums as you type. See The launcher.
  • Right-click the desktop, the bar or the dock for a menu of what you can do there (Right-click menus).
  • Settings (Super + Z) has every setting in one place, and every setting is searchable. See The Settings app.

A few things to set

Slate works without any of these, but you might like to:

  • Set your location in  Settings → General, for the weather and a night light from sunset to sunrise.
  • Pick a wallpaper (Super + Shift + W). With colours from the wallpaper on, everything follows it. See Wallpaper.
  • Check your displays in Settings → Displays, and how big Slate itself is in Appearance → Interface size.
  • Add your keyboard layouts in Settings → Input.
