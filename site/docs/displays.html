Your computer

Displays

Slate picks up every screen you plug in and gives each one its own menu bar. When you'd like things a little different, everything about your screens is in one place, and nothing you change sticks until you say so.

Arranging your screens

Open  Settings → Displays. With more than one screen connected, the top of the page shows them as tiles, to scale, laid out as they are now. Drag a tile beside another to match where the screen really is, so the pointer crosses from one to the next where you'd expect. The main display is marked Main.

Click a tile to choose that screen. Its settings appear underneath, headed with its name.

A screen's settings

SettingWhat it does
Use this displayTurn a screen off to leave it dark and unused. The only screen in use can't be turned off, so you're never left without one.
ResolutionThe sizes the screen reports. Laptop panels often offer only their own, so to make things larger or smaller there, use Scale instead.
Refresh rateHow many times a second the picture updates, from the rates this resolution has. Higher is smoother.
ScaleHow large everything on this screen looks, apps included. Only scales that suit the resolution are offered.
RotationFor a screen turned on its side or upside down.
HDROnly for screens that support it. Auto uses HDR while a game or video fills the screen with HDR content, On keeps it on all the time in 10-bit colour, and Off never uses it.
SDR brightness and SDR colourWith HDR on, how bright and how colourful everything that isn't HDR looks: most apps, and the desktop.
Main displayWhere the main menu bar lives. Every screen has a bar, and the launcher and other panels open on the screen you're using.

When you pick a new resolution, Slate keeps the refresh rate if the new size has it (or takes its fastest), and moves the scale to the nearest one that suits it.

Keeping a change

A new resolution or refresh rate can leave a screen blank if it doesn't really suit it. So whenever you change something, Slate asks Keep these display settings? with a countdown. Choose Keep if everything looks right, or Go back to undo it straight away. If you can't see the question at all, just wait: when the countdown runs out, Slate puts things back as they were by itself.

Making Slate bigger or smaller

A screen's scale changes everything on it, your apps included. If you'd rather make just Slate larger or smaller (the bar, the dock, the panels and the Settings window) on every screen at once, use Interface size in  Settings → Appearance. It works on top of each screen's scale. See Appearance.

Brightness

Your keyboard's brightness keys change the screen's brightness (and the keyboard's backlight, where it has keys for it), with a small pop-up showing the new level, just like the volume.

Your own monitor rules

If you'd rather set your screens up by hand, rules in ~/.config/slate/hypr/monitors.lua take priority over the Displays page. Slate never overwrites that file. See Your own config.
