The desktop

Right-click menus

Right-click almost anything in Slate for a short menu of what you can do with it. Every menu looks and behaves the same way, so once you know one, you know them all.

How they work

  • Each menu points at what it's for, with a small pointer on its side: the dock's at the icon, the bar's just off the bar, the desktop's at the cursor. Near the edge of the screen it flips or slides along to stay in view.
  • Submenus open to the side. An item with an arrow at its end has more choices. Point at it (or click it) and they open beside the menu, to the right, or to the left when there isn't room.
  • Your current choice is ticked, like the dock's position or the clock's format, and switches like Auto-hide are ticked while they're on.
  • Things that can't be undone turn red when you point at them, like Force Quit or emptying the waste basket.
  • The keys work them too. ↑ and ↓ move along the items, Enter or Space chooses one, → goes into a submenu and ← comes back out.
  • They get out of your way. Click outside the menu or press Esc to close it. It also closes when you change workspace, or open the launcher, a switcher or Nova.

The desktop

Right-click the wallpaper:

  • App launcher, Terminal and Files.
  • Change wallpaper…, which opens the wallpaper settings, and Next wallpaper.
  • Display settings and Settings.

The menu bar

Right-click an empty part of the bar:

  • Auto-hide, ticked while it's on.
  • Position: Top, Bottom, Left or Right.
  • Clock: 12-hour or 24-hour, and Seconds.
  • Edit layout, to arrange the bar (The menu bar).
  • Menu bar settings.

The control centre

Right-click Slate's mark at the start of the bar:

  • Settings, Appearance and Next wallpaper.
  • Do Not Disturb, Night Light and Glass, ticked while they're on.
  • Check for updates (or Update to the new version, when there is one), Restart Slate and Show the welcome.
  • Lock screen, Log out… and About Slate.

Log out asks before it does anything. Restart Slate restarts only the shell, so your apps and windows stay open.

The dock

Apps

Right-click an app on the dock:

  • Its open windows, to go to one. The one you're in has a dot.
  • The app's own actions (a new private window and so on) and New Window.
  • Pin to Dock or Unpin from Dock.
  • Close Window (or Close 2 Windows, and so on), for every window it has open.
  • Force Quit, for an app that's stopped responding. Anything it hasn't saved is lost.

The Apps button

Settings, then a few of its pages: Appearance, Wallpaper, Displays, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

What's playing

Play or Pause, Next, Previous, and Show the app that's playing.

Downloads

Open Downloads, to open the folder. (A left click shows a grid of your newest downloads instead.)

The waste basket

Open, and Empty Waste Basket… when there's something in it. Emptying deletes it all for good, so Slate asks first.

The dock's empty space

  • Auto-hide, ticked while it's on.
  • Position: Top, Bottom, Left or Right.
  • Hover effect: Magnify, Tilt, Magnify and tilt, Fade, Fade and magnify, Glow, Glow and magnify, Raise or None.
  • Icon size: Small, Medium or Large.
  • Show: the Apps button, What's playing, Downloads and the Waste Basket, each ticked while it's on the dock.
  • Dock settings.

App tray menus

Right-click an app's icon in the bar's tray for that app's own menu, drawn in Slate's style. Its submenus open in place, with a row at the top to go back. A left click does the app's main action, and a middle click its other one; for apps that only have a menu, any click opens it.
