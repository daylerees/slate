The desktop

The menu bar

The bar along the top of your screen holds your workspaces, the window you're in, and everything about your computer at a glance. Click anything on it for a panel with more.

What's on it

Out of the box, from left to right:

  • The control centre, Slate's mark: your quick switches, media, the weather and more. See Control centre and panels.
  • Apps, which opens the launcher, and Nova's button when the assistant is switched on (Nova).
  • Spaces, for saving and bringing back sets of workspaces (Spaces).
  • The workspace's window mode and your workspaces as dots, the one you're on lit in your accent colour.
  • The window you're in: its app's icon and its title.
  • On the right: app tray icons (evened up in size, whatever margin an app draws round its icon), status icons (notifications, keyboard layout, Bluetooth, volume, Wi-Fi and battery), the clock and the power button.

A few more are off until you put them there: clipboard history, screenshots, a camera indicator (an open eye while an app is using your camera) and game mode. You add them in Edit layout.

Panels

Click almost anything on the bar and its panel slides out of the bar, joined to it by curved corners: volume and the output device, Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth devices, the battery and power mode, the calendar and weather under the clock, and so on. Each panel ends with a link to its page in Settings.

A button answers anywhere across the bar, from the screen's edge to the bar's own edge, not only on the shape drawn in the middle, so you can push your pointer right up against the edge of the screen and click.

Click anywhere outside a panel, press Esc, or change workspace to close it. Opening the launcher, Nova or a switcher closes it too.

Scroll over the volume to change it, and over the keyboard layout to switch to the next one.

Where it goes

The bar can sit on any edge of the screen: top, bottom, left or right. Change it in  Settings → Menu Bar, or right-click an empty part of the bar and choose Position. It slides off its old edge and back in at the new one. If the dock was on that edge, the dock moves to the opposite one.

On the left or right, the status icons drop their text and the window's title turns to run along the bar.

Auto-hide

Switch on Auto-hide (in Settings, or from the bar's right-click menu) and the bar steps out of the way when a window needs the room. Push the pointer to the screen's edge and it comes back. It stays while you're using it or a panel is open.

Arranging it your way

Choose Edit layout from the bar's right-click menu, or in  Settings → Menu Bar. The bar itself becomes the editor, with everything behind it dimmed:

  • Drag anything along the bar. A gap opens where it will land.
  • Containers hold things together. Drag things in and out, move a whole container by its gripper, split one up, or add an empty one.
  • Flexible spaces take up the spare room and push what's either side apart. The bar always keeps at least one.
  • What you don't use waits in a pool under the bar. Drag it back, or click it, when you want it.
  • Click anything for its options: whether it shows its text, the clock's date and seconds, how a container collapses.

Press Esc or Done when you're finished. Defaults puts everything back as Slate first had it.

With the assistant switched on, you can also ask Nova (or another assistant) to arrange it for you, in words: "put the clock on the left", "tuck Wi-Fi and volume behind one button". It can't show or hide Nova's own button, and you can change anything it does here afterwards.

When it doesn't all fit

On a smaller screen, at a bigger interface size, or with a long network name, the bar slims itself down rather than running off the end. The window's title shortens and then goes, the text beside icons goes (longest first), and the clock sheds its date and seconds. Everything comes back as soon as there's room.

  • Containers can collapse behind a button of their own: Never, Always, or When needed (how they start), which tucks one away only when the bar's short of room.
  • More holds whatever still doesn't fit. You place it like anything else, and its panel lists each thing with its value.
  • Set priority, in Edit layout, decides what leaves first: drag the list into order, and what's at the top stays longest. Some things always stay, Power, the clock and the control centre to start with, and you can choose others.
  • Try it shorter shows your bar at three quarters, half or a third of its length, in the middle of the screen's edge, so you can see what would happen.

How it looks

With glass on (Appearance), the bar has sliders of its own in  Settings → Menu Bar: how see-through its background is, whether what's behind it is blurred, and how solid its containers are, normally and under the pointer. Its panels have their own opacity and blur too.

When you point at the bar or open a panel, a shadow appears beneath it. It follows your shadow settings.

Right-click menu

Right-click an empty part of the bar for Auto-hide, Position, Clock (12 or 24-hour, and seconds), Edit layout and the bar's settings. See Right-click menus.
