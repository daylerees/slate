Reference

Moving your settings

Settings → Import & Export keeps your Slate settings in one file: to keep safe, or to take to another computer so it looks and works the way you're used to.

What's in the file

  • Every setting from the Settings app.
  • Your saved Spaces, and the apps pinned to your dock.
  • Your own files: your Hyprland tweaks (~/.config/slate/hypr) and your own QML (user/).
  • Your wallpaper picture, so the other computer looks the same. If it's one of Slate's own wallpapers, it's named rather than copied, as every Slate has it.

Workspace names last only until you log out, so they aren't saved. Hidden files in your own folders (a .git folder, an editor's swap files) are left out too.

Export

Choose Export and Slate saves a file called Slate settings with today's date to your Documents folder; Show opens the folder. The file holds your own files too, so keep it somewhere private.

Import

Put the file (it ends in .slate-settings) in your Downloads or Documents folder, on the Desktop or in your home folder, and it shows up under Import. Choose Import… beside it, and Slate asks before anything changes.

Importing replaces your settings, saved Spaces, own files and wallpaper with the ones in the file, then Slate restarts to use them. Your apps and windows stay open. A settings file can change which apps open things, how your screen locks and what your keys do, and your own files can hold code that runs as part of your desktop, so only import a file you made or trust. Slate only reads what belongs in a settings file, and leaves a file with anything else in it alone, saying why.

If an import can't finish, Slate puts back everything it had changed, and a notification says so.

Undoing an import

What an import replaces is kept first. Undo import… on the same page puts it all back as it was, your own folders included (one that wasn't there before goes again). It undoes the latest import, once. The copies stay in ~/.local/share/slate/backups either way.
