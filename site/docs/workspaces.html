Windows and workspaces

Workspaces and the overview

Workspaces give each thing you're doing a screen of its own. Slate keeps them in a row: move along it with a key, see them all at once in the overview, and keep a secret one tucked away above the rest.

Moving between workspaces

KeysWhat they do
Super + ← / →The previous or next workspace. Super and the scroll wheel does the same.
Super + Alt + 1–9Go to that workspace.
Super + Ctrl + ← / →Take the window you're in to the previous or next workspace.
Super + WMove the window to a new workspace after the last one in use.
Super + Shift + Alt + 1–9Send the window to that workspace, staying where you are.

You can also click a workspace's dot on the menu bar. With more than one display, each has its own workspaces: Super + Ctrl + 1–9 goes to this display's workspace, and Super + 1–9 moves to another display. The full list is in Keyboard shortcuts.

As you move, an indicator near the bottom of the screen shows your workspace dots and the name of the one you land on. When you open a link from another app, Slate takes you to the workspace your browser is on.

Wrapping round

Switch on Workspaces wrap around in  Settings → Workspaces → Switching and going past the last workspace brings you back to the first, and the other way. It's off to start with, so the row simply stops at its ends.

An empty one, always ready

Keep an empty workspace, on to start with, keeps an empty workspace after the last one in use, ready and waiting for something new.

Names

Click the window mode button beside the workspace dots on the menu bar, then the pencil next to the workspace's name. Give it a short name, like "Coding" or "Music", and it takes the place of "Workspace 3" in the overview, the indicator, the bar's hover text and the Spaces panel. A name stays for as long as the workspace is around, and it's saved with a space. Leave the name empty to go back to the number.

You can also keep the name on the menu bar: drag Workspace name onto it in Edit layout. It shows the workspace you're on, and clicking it opens the same panel, pencil and all.

The overview

Press Super + ↓ to see every workspace as a large card, with its windows on it.

  • Choose one with ← / → (or Super + ← / →), then press Enter or ↑, or just click its card.
  • Rearrange them: hold Shift to lift the chosen card, then use the arrow keys to carry it along the row. Everything on it goes with it: its windows, laid out as they were, its name and its window mode.
  • Close it with Esc, or click outside the cards.

In  Settings → Workspaces → Workspace overview (Super+Down):

SettingChoices
Windows showLive previews, App icons (each window's outline with its app's icon, nothing captured) or Both.
Moving between workspacesSlide, Hop, Wheel, Tilt, Orbit, Deck, Fan, Grid or Instant. Grid lays every workspace out in rows; the arrow keys move round it, and more rows than fit scroll.
Card sizeHow wide the cards are. With Grid, Grid size instead: how many go across, from 2 × 2 to 9 × 9.
Going to the workspaceZoom (the card grows until it becomes the workspace), Fade, Workspace animation or Instant.

The secret workspace

The secret workspace sits over the others, out of sight until you want it: a quiet spot for a music player, a chat window, or anything you'd like kept out of the way.

  • Super + ↑ (or Super + S) shows or hides it.
  • Super + Shift + S sends the window you're in there.

When it's empty, a note in the middle of the screen says so. Its windows stay out of the window switcher and the overview.

Indicators

Your workspaces show as dots on the menu bar, in the window switcher, in the overview and in the indicator as you switch, with the one you're on lit in your accent colour.  Settings → Workspaces → Look → Workspace indicators draws them as Circles or Squares, and Name when switching (in Switching) turns the name under the indicator on or off.

Lots of workspaces

The menu bar shows up to six dots, and the switcher, the overview and the indicator up to ten. With more workspaces than that, the row scrolls smoothly, keeping the one you're on in the middle, and the dots at an end with more beyond it shrink and fade away, so you can see there's more that way. When you reach the first or last workspace, the row stops and the marker moves on to the end.

Switching animation

Workspace switching, in Settings → Workspaces → Switching, sets how one workspace gives way to the next: Slide Fade (the default, the new one sliding in over the old as it fades), Slide, Crossfade, Vertical Slide Fade, Vertical Slide or None. While a switch slides, windows go square at the corners so the desktop never peeks through between them. With animations off, workspaces switch straight away.
