Look and feel

App theming

Your apps can wear Slate's colours too: light or dark, and your accent, even one picked from your wallpaper. It's up to you, app by app, and switching one off puts it back the way it was.

Which apps

KindApps
Desktop appsGTK apps (Files, Text Editor, Calculator and other GNOME and GTK apps), Qt apps (through qt6ct), KDE apps (Dolphin, Kate and others), GNOME Text Editor, Firefox (toolbars, tabs and menus), Discord (Vesktop, Vencord and Equicord), Spotify (with Spicetify) and Obsidian (a CSS snippet in each vault)
Terminalskitty, Alacritty, Ghostty, foot and WezTerm. Each gets a see-through starting config if it has none.
Editors and toolsVS Code (Code - OSS and VSCodium too), Zed, Helix, Vim and Neovim, bat, fzf, Yazi, btop, cava and zathura

Flatpak and Snap builds are themed too (see below). Text colours are kept readable against each background, in light mode as well as dark, and terminals and editors use your code font.

Switching an app on

Open  Settings → App Theming. Every app Slate can theme has a row saying what it is and what Slate does to it, with a link to its Website, an Open button when it's installed, and a switch. Nothing is themed until you switch it on.

Once an app is on, it follows Slate from then on: change your accent, switch between light and dark, or let a new wallpaper bring new colours, and the app changes with it. Most apps pick up the change while they're open; a few need a restart, and their row says so (Firefox, for example, and Obsidian if it was open).

Flatpak, Snap and other ways of installing

How an app was installed changes where it keeps its settings. Here's how each kind gets Slate's colours:

Installed fromWhat Slate does
Your distribution, or an AppImageThey read your own settings, so they're themed like everything else.
FlatpakGTK and KDE apps are allowed to read Slate's theme files, and only read them: one entry in Flatpak's settings for all your apps, the same as flatpak override --user --filesystem=xdg-config/gtk-4.0:ro. Access you've already given or refused stays as it is, and switching the app off takes Slate's entry away again. Apps with their own theme (VS Code, Zed, Discord, Firefox and the others above) are themed in their Flatpak's own folder. Restart a Flatpak app that was open to see it.
SnapGTK apps get Slate's theme files in their own folder (~/snap/<app>/current/.config). Snaps only have the GTK themes that come with them, so older GTK 3 apps keep Adwaita's shapes, in Slate's colours.
Home Manager (NixOS)Files Home Manager looks after are read-only, so Slate leaves them as they are and notes it in its log. Add Slate's line to your Home Manager configuration instead (for GTK, @import url("slate.css"); in gtk.css). A config that's a link from another dotfile manager stays a link, and Slate changes the file it leads to.

A few apps can't wear Slate's colours, whichever way they're installed:

  • Qt apps from Flatpak, Snap or an AppImage bring their own Qt without qt6ct, so they keep their own look. KDE apps from Flatpak do follow Slate, through KDE's colours.
  • Chrome, Chromium and other Electron apps follow light or dark, but not your accent. Discord, VS Code and Obsidian, above, have Slate themes of their own.
  • Light or dark in Flatpak and Snap apps comes through xdg-desktop-portal-gtk. The installer adds it where your distribution has it; without it, those apps stay light.

Backups and switching off

Before Slate first changes one of your config files, it saves a copy beside it, ending in .bak-slate-theme. Slate keeps its colours in theme files of its own and points your config at them, noting every change it makes.

Switch an app off and Slate undoes exactly what it did: its theme files go, its lines come out of your config, and any line of yours it replaced is put back. Your own edits stay as they are.

Installing an app

An app that isn't on your computer has an Install button, when your distribution has it. Slate asks first and says which packages it will add, then installs them from your distribution's own software, which asks for your password. A notification says when it's done, and you can switch on its theme straight away.

Refreshing

Sometimes an app update or a reset overwrites a theme. Refresh, at the top of the page, puts the themes of every app you've switched on back in place.



Spotify and Discord updates remove their themes (Spicetify's and Vencord's patches). Slate notices within a few minutes, puts them back, and tells you to restart the app. Where it can't, it says so once.
