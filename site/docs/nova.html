The desktop

Nova, the assistant

Nova is Slate's own way to ask for things in plain words: "turn on glass", "put Firefox on workspace 2", "where do I change the font?". An AI agent you already use does it, through Slate's own tools. It's off until you switch it on.

What it is

Nova isn't an AI of Slate's own. It hands what you type to an agent you already have and are signed in to, like Claude Code, and that agent works through a set of tools Slate gives it. Slate keeps no keys and sends nothing anywhere itself: your prompts go to the agent's service under your own account, and count towards it like any other.

Nova is still an experiment, and its window says so.

Switching it on

Go to  Settings → Assistant and turn on Let assistants use Slate. Before anything is switched on, Slate tells you what it means and asks you to choose Switch on:

  • What leaves your computer. What you type, and what Slate's tools tell the agent about your desktop (your settings, your open windows and their titles, the names of your workspaces, Spaces, apps and wallpapers), goes to the service behind your agent.
  • What it can do. Change settings, move windows and open apps without asking. Slate asks you before anything that can't be undone.
  • What can go wrong. Assistants make mistakes, and can be misled by text they read, like a web page's title. Slate does its best to keep one within these limits, but it can't promise what one will do, so using it is your choice and your responsibility.

Read the risks again shows them at any time, with a button to switch off. If a later version of Slate changes what the risks are, Nova pauses until you've read the new ones.

Choosing your agent

There's no agent to start with: pick one under Agent on the same page, and what it needs shows beneath it.

  • Claude Code is the recommended one. For each prompt, Slate switches off Claude Code's own tools (its shell, files and web access) and any other servers you've added, so it works through Slate's tools alone. Nothing in your own Claude Code setup changes. If it isn't installed or signed in yet, Settings says so and offers Get it or Sign in.
  • A command of your own runs whatever command you type, with your words on its input. Slate can't limit what such a command does, so it's marked "Keeps its own tools" and choosing it asks you first.

Model picks a model for agents that let you choose; left empty, the agent uses its own.

Asking Nova

Open Nova with any of these:

  • Super + K.
  • Your keyboard's Copilot or assistant key, if it has one.
  • Nova's button on the menu bar, which appears while it's switched on.

A small window opens, like the launcher. Type what you want in a line and press Enter. You can change the shortcuts in Shortcuts.

The window

  • It reads like a chat: your messages on the right in your accent colour, Nova's on the left, each with when it was said. Answers appear as they're written.
  • What it did is listed under each answer. That list is Slate's own record, in Slate's words, not the agent's account of itself.
  • Copy a message by pointing at it: a copy button appears in its corner.
  • Following on just works. What you type within five minutes of the last answer carries on from it ("and a bit bigger"). Left longer, Nova quietly starts afresh with the agent. There are no conversations to manage, and your history stays on show either way.
  • Close it with Esc or a click outside, and the work carries on: the button on the bar turns while Nova's busy, and a notification brings the answer when it's done.
  • Stop ends it at any time. Locking the screen stops it too.

If something goes wrong, Nova says what happened and offers what will help: Try again, Open a terminal (to sign in to your agent) or Open Settings.

Chat history

Your last 100 messages are still there after Slate restarts, kept on this computer only and never sent anywhere. Turn Keep chat history off and it's forgotten whenever Slate restarts (what was kept is deleted). Clear chat history empties the window; nothing Nova did is undone.

What it can and can't do

Without asking

Looking things up: your settings, your open windows and workspaces, your Spaces and apps, what's playing, and so on. Changes that can be put back also happen straight away: changing a setting, moving a window, opening an app or a Space, the wallpaper, volume and brightness.

Always asking first

Closing a window, deleting a Space, and logging out, restarting, shutting down or sleeping always ask, in Slate's own confirmation window, with Cancel picked first. The assistant can't write the question. A question nobody answers goes away after 45 seconds and nothing happens. After a no, Slate won't ask about the same thing again for ten minutes.

Turn on Ask before changing anything and Slate asks about every change, not only the ones that can't be undone.

What it never does

Slate's tools can't run a command, open a file or a web address, type or click for you, read your clipboard, answer a password prompt, unlock the screen, or install anything. They answer in words only: they never show an assistant your screen, your files or your clipboard. While the screen is locked, every tool is refused.

Some settings are yours alone, and Slate refuses to let an assistant change them: what the assistant may do, locking, keyboard layouts and the shortcut set, default and startup apps, folders and your location.



Slate can only answer for its own tools. An agent with tools of its own, like a command of your own, can do whatever those tools allow, and Slate can't limit that.

What it can see

Under What it can see in Settings:

  • Window titles (on to start with). Titles can carry document names and web page titles; off, assistants get app names only.
  • Notifications (off to start with). On, assistants can read your recent notifications.

See what Slate adds shows exactly what travels with your words: who the agent is here, and a few lines about the moment, like the time and the window you're in. It never includes your name, your computer's name or your home folder.

Private Mode

While Private Mode is on, Nova and every assistant are off, and Nova's window says so. Only you can turn Private Mode off, from the control centre or the launcher, and Nova is back as it was when you do. An assistant can't change Private Mode.
