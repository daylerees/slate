The desktop

Control centre and panels

The control centre is the first thing on the menu bar, Slate's mark. It gathers the things you reach for most in one place, and every other item on the bar opens a panel of its own.

Opening it

Click Slate's mark at the start of the menu bar, or press Super + X. Click anywhere outside it or press Esc to close it again.

What's in it

Your profile

Across the top: your name and picture over your wallpaper, your user and computer name, and how long it's been running. Point at your picture and click it to choose a new one, in a picker of its own with your picture folders, a grid of thumbnails and a round preview.

Beside it are two buttons: one opens Settings, the other the power menu (lock, suspend, log out, restart and shut down).

The time and weather

A card with the time and date, and the weather as it is now. When nothing's playing there's room for the next few days too. The weather shows once Slate knows where you are: set your location in  Settings → General → Location.

What's playing

When music or a video is playing, its artwork, title and artist appear under the clock, with Previous, Play/Pause and Next.

Quick switches

On the right are eight quick switches, two to a row, all the same size. Each one lights up while it's on; click it to switch it. These are the ones there are:

SwitchWhat it does
Wi-Fi, BluetoothTurns the radio on or off.
Airplane ModeWi-Fi, Bluetooth and mobile broadband off together, and back as they were after.
CaffeineKeeps your computer awake: no locking or sleeping while it's on.
Night LightWarmer colours for the evening (Night light).
Do Not DisturbHolds back notification pop-ups (Do Not Disturb).
Performance, Power SaverThe power mode, as in the battery panel.
Game ModeTurns off what costs the most to draw, for smoother games.
Light ModeSwitches between Slate's light and dark looks.
Mute, Mute MicYour speakers or your microphone.
GlassSee-through, frosted surfaces (Appearance).
Charging LimitStops your battery charging all the way, where your laptop supports it. Charging to full again asks first.
Hide Dock, Hide Menu BarAuto-hide for the dock or the bar.
FloatingFloating windows on the workspace you're on; off puts it back as it was.
ClipboardWhether Slate keeps a history of what you copy (Clipboard).
Private ModeMicrophones muted, the camera off for every app, Nova off, and screenshots and screen sharing stopped, all back as they were when you turn it off (Privacy).

Switches for hardware your computer hasn't got, like Bluetooth or a battery, are left out, and the next one along takes the place. If the hardware turns up later, the switch comes back.



The first time you turn on Private Mode, Slate asks before adding the small helper that turns the camera off. It needs your password once.

Choosing your switches

Out of the box you get Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Airplane Mode, Caffeine, Night Light, Do Not Disturb, Performance and Game Mode. To change them, go to  Settings → Control Centre. It shows a picture of the panel's switches, with the spare ones waiting beside them under More switches:

  • Drag one switch onto another and the two change places.
  • Drag one across from More switches onto a switch in the panel to put it in; the one it replaces goes back to wait.

You can change this at any time, and your order is kept.

Right-click menu

Right-click the control centre's button for a short menu of Slate's own:

  • Settings, Appearance and Next wallpaper.
  • Do Not Disturb, Night Light and Glass, ticked while they're on.
  • Check for updates (or Update to the new version when there is one), Restart Slate and Show the welcome.
  • Lock screen, Log out… (which asks first) and About Slate.

Restarting Slate only restarts the shell: your apps and windows stay open.

The bar's other panels

Most things on the menu bar open a panel when you click them. Each slides out of the bar beside its button, and ends with a link to its page in Settings. Click outside it, press Esc or change workspace to close it.

Volume

A slider each for your output and your microphone, with a button to mute either, and a list of output devices when you have more than one. Stripes drift along a slider while sound plays through it or something is listening to it. Scroll over the volume icon on the bar to change it without opening the panel.

Wi-Fi

A switch, then the networks around you: click one to join it, and type its password if it asks. The network you're on shows its signal and details, a VPN when one's connected, and Disconnect. On a cable, the icon turns to a wired one and the panel shows the cable's details. See Network.

Bluetooth

A switch and your devices: click one to connect or disconnect it.

Battery and power

Your charge, then the power mode: Saver, Balanced or Performance. Where your laptop reports them, you'll also see the battery's health and the level it charges up to. On a computer without a battery it's a power button instead, with the power mode and a few system readings. See Power.

Keyboard layout

Your keyboard layouts, to switch between. Scroll over the bar's layout to step to the next one.

The clock

A month calendar with the weather forecast for the days ahead.

Notifications

Your notification history, a Do Not Disturb switch and a button to clear them all. See Notifications.

Clipboard

The latest things you copied: click one to copy it again. Show all and search (Super + V) opens the full list, and Clear history empties it. Clipboard history is off the bar until you add it.

Screenshot

Take a screenshot of an Area, a Window or the Screen. The panel gets out of the way first, so it isn't in the picture. See Screenshots.

Game mode

A switch, and the list of what game mode turns off: glass and blur, see-through windows, shadows, animations, live window previews (shapes show instead), notification pop-ups (they still go to the list) and ignoring the touchpad while you type. Turning it off puts everything back as you had it.

Window mode

The button before the workspace dots shows how the workspace you're on arranges its windows: Tiling, Side Stack, Scrolling, Stacking, Solo or Floating. Its panel lets you pick one for this workspace only, or Default to follow the setting for every workspace. The pencil beside its name lets you rename the workspace. See Windows.

Spaces, More and Power

Spaces saves and brings back sets of workspaces (Spaces). More holds whatever doesn't fit on a crowded bar (The menu bar). Power, at the end of the bar (or Super + Alt + C), has Lock, Suspend, Log out, Restart and Shut down; anything that ends your session asks first.



When you restart, a switch on the question (on to start with) reopens your apps afterwards, each window on the same workspace and in the same place.
