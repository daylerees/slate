Apps and tools

Software

Find and install apps from Flathub, and keep your apps and your computer's system up to date, all in one place.

Opening Software

Search for Software in the launcher (Super). It opens on Discover, with the search box ready for you to type. From a script, it's slate ipc call software open (see The slate command).

Apps come from Flathub, where thousands of free apps run safely in a space of their own (Flatpak). Software needs the flatpak package and Flathub set up for it (the installer sets up Flathub for you): if either is missing, it says so and how to add it. The first time, Software gets Flathub's list of apps, which takes a few seconds; if you're offline, it says so, with Try again.

Finding apps

Discover has a large search box in the middle: type a name or what you're after ("photo", "music player") and matching apps show beneath it as you type. Search works from any page, and offline too, since the catalog is kept on your computer.

Below the search, each category (Games, Graphics & Photos, Music & Video and so on) has a row of its most installed apps on Flathub. Move along a row with its arrows, or click See all for the whole category, its popular apps first. The popular lists are fetched from Flathub at most every six hours; offline, the rows are in alphabetical order.

An app's page

Click an app to see its page: what it does, who makes it, its version and license, its screenshots and a description, with Install (or Open and Remove once it's installed). Click a screenshot to see it large: Left and Right (or the arrows) step through the rest, and Esc, the close button or a click outside closes it. Back, beside the app's name, returns you to where you were.

Installing and removing

Click Install. Apps are installed for everyone on the computer, so this may ask for an administrator's password. A progress bar shows how far the download and install have got. Several installs wait their turn and run one at a time. Once installed, an app is in the launcher straight away. If it's the first app Flatpak has ever installed here, it can land in a folder Slate wasn't watching yet: Slate then asks to restart so the app shows, and your apps and windows stay open.

Closing Software doesn't stop an install, and neither does restarting Slate: open Software again and it's still there, with its progress.

To remove an app, click Remove on its page or on Installed, then Remove again to confirm. It's removed for everyone; your own files from it stay where they are, and you can install it again any time.

Installed

Installed lists the apps you have from Flathub, each with Open and Remove.

Updates

Updates shows a count of what's waiting in the sidebar. Software looks each time it opens, and Check again looks once more.

WhatHow it updates
Your appsUpdate all apps, or Update beside one to update it on its own. Your apps and windows stay open while they update.
This computer's systemIts packages are listed with the version each goes to, and update together with Update (one package on its own could leave the system half updated). It asks for an administrator's password. Restart afterwards if it says to, for a new kernel, say.

When nothing's waiting, Updates says you're up to date, and when it last looked.

You don't need to keep checking: once a day Slate looks by itself and, if anything's waiting, tells you with a notification that opens Updates. Switch it off with Tell me about app and system updates in  Settings → Updates.



System updates use your distro's own tools: checkupdates on Arch (the pacman-contrib package), PackageKit where it's installed, or apt, dnf or zypper. On NixOS, your configuration updates the system, so Software only lists apps there.

Slate's own updates are separate: see Updates.

How it looks

Software follows your theme and the rest of Slate's look. Apps' icons are drawn the way the dock draws them, in the shape and style you chose in  Settings → Icons.
