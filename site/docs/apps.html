Apps and tools

Default and startup apps

Tell Slate which apps you like for the everyday things, and which you'd like waiting for you each time you log in.

Default apps

In  Settings → Default Apps, choose an app for each of these. Slate's shortcuts and menus use them, and so do links and files you open:

AppUsed for
TerminalSuper + Return.
Web browserLinks you open.
FilesSuper + E, and folders you open.
Text editorSuper + T, and text files you open.
CalculatorSuper + C, and your keyboard's calculator key.

Each list offers the apps you have installed that can do the job. Anything you leave unchosen uses Slate's own choice, or the one set in ~/.config/slate/hypr/variables.lua if you've set one there (Your own config).

Quick Look in your file manager

In Nautilus (Files) and Nemo, press Space on a file to see it in Quick Look, and ← or → in Nautilus to go to the next file. This needs nothing set up. If GNOME Sushi or nemo-preview is installed, they keep doing it instead.

For Dolphin and Thunar, switch on Quick Look in Dolphin and Thunar (shown when one of them is installed). It adds Quick Look to their right-click menus, and to Space in Thunar. Switch it off and Slate takes it out again, leaving the rest of their settings as they were.



Dolphin keeps Space for selecting files, so Quick Look is on its right-click menu. You can give it a key of your own in Dolphin's Settings → Configure Keyboard Shortcuts.

Startup apps

In  Settings → Startup Apps, Add an app and pick one from the list. It opens each time you log in. Your startup apps are listed under At login, and you can remove one with the button beside it; Slate asks first, and you can always add it again.

When your apps from last time come back too, each startup app opens on a workspace of its own, so it doesn't crowd the windows coming back.



Anything started from your own ~/.config/slate/hypr/autostart.lua still opens too, alongside the apps listed here.

Your apps from last time

As you log out, restart or shut down, Slate keeps a note of the apps you had open: which workspace and screen each window was on, and whether it was tiled or floating. At your next login it can bring them all back, just where you left them, and return you to the workspace you were on.

Reopen your apps, in  Settings → Startup Apps → Your apps from last time, says how:

ChoiceWhat happens as you log in
AskA notification asks Pick up where you left off? Reopen brings them back; Not now starts fresh. Nothing opens unless you say so. This is the default.
AlwaysThey come back by themselves.
NeverSlate doesn't keep them, and every login starts fresh.

Your startup apps open either way. Apps reopen fresh, as when you start them yourself: a terminal comes back empty, while apps that restore their own tabs and documents (most browsers and editors) do that too. Apps kept more than a month ago aren't offered.

Opening apps

To find and open any app, use the launcher (Super on its own, or Super + Space), or keep your favorites on the dock.
