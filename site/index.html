What is Slate?

Slate is a desktop shell for Hyprland, the tiling Wayland compositor, built with Quickshell (QML). It adds everything you'd expect from a desktop around Hyprland's window management, in one consistent dark-slate design with the Geist font and Tabler icons.

It installs as its own Slate session, so your existing Hyprland setup is left alone: pick Slate on the login screen to use it, or your old desktop to go back. Everything is set from Settings, and anything you'd rather write by hand still goes in your own config files, which Slate never overwrites.

Slate is inspired by the best parts of macOS, Windows and Linux, and blends them together into something fresh. Things work the way you'd expect, so it's a welcoming place for people new to Linux and experienced users alike.

Everything a desktop needs



Menu bar

Workspaces, the window title, app tray icons (tuck them away behind one button), notifications, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, keyboard layout, volume, battery and the clock. Every item drops down its own panel. Top, bottom or either side.



Magnifying dock

Pinned and running apps that grow under the pointer, bounce while they open, and have right-click menus for their windows and actions. Hides when a window needs the room.



Launcher

Tap Super and type. A list or a grid of apps, sorted by how often you use them; every setting is searchable from it too, and it does sums. Open apps on a new workspace with a key.



Switchers and overview

Super+Tab switches windows with live previews; Super+Down shows every workspace as a large live card to jump to or rearrange. Super alone opens the launcher, and every shortcut is listed and searchable in Settings.



Control centre, panels and notifications

Your profile, media, the weather and quick toggles, plus panels for networks, devices, sound, battery and the calendar. Notifications pop up where you want them, with a history and Do not disturb.



Theming that follows your wallpaper

Light, dark or automatic by sunset. The accent and background tint can come from the wallpaper, which can change by itself. App icons come in tile, squircle, circle and more.



Displays

Arrange monitors by dragging, pick resolution, rotation and per-monitor scale, choose the main display, and size Slate itself. Changes revert by themselves unless you keep them.



Lock and login screens

A matching lock screen and greetd login screen with your wallpaper and picture. Lock, turn the screen off or suspend when you're away, and lock before sleep.



A Settings app for everything

Appearance, displays, keyboard and mouse, sound, power, windows, shortcuts, default and startup apps, date and time, and more, with search across them all.



The little things

Night light on a schedule, hot corners, clipboard history, an emoji picker, screenshots, a right-click desktop menu, and password prompts that look the part.



Your apps, in Slate's colours

Switch on the apps you want to match: GTK and Qt apps, kitty, Alacritty, Ghostty, foot, WezTerm, VS Code, Zed, Helix, Vim, bat, fzf, Yazi, btop, Discord, Spotify, Firefox and more. They follow light, dark and your accent. Configs are backed up, and switching an app off puts it back.



Tiling or traditional windows

Windows tile themselves by default. Prefer the classic way? Switch to floating windows with title bars you can drag, plus close and maximise buttons (and double-click to maximise), in Settings → Windows.

Theming

Colours that follow your wallpaper

Pick a wallpaper and Slate takes its accent and a gentle tint from it: the bar, panels, Settings and the apps you've switched on all shift together, light or dark. The wallpaper can change by itself, on a timer or at login.

Tiling

Windows that arrange themselves

Open an app and Slate makes room for it: no dragging, no overlapping. Move focus, swap windows, flip a split or resize, all from the keyboard, and everything slides smoothly into place. Prefer the classic way? Floating windows with title bars are a switch away.

Launcher

Tap Super and type

Your apps, most used first, narrowing as you type. Every setting is searchable from here too, and it does sums: type one and the answer is ready to copy.

Control centre

Everything at a glance

Your profile, the time and the week's weather, with quick toggles for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, caffeine, night light, Do not disturb and power mode. One click on the slate in the corner of the bar.

Menu bar

Every item opens a panel

Power, the calendar and forecast, battery and power mode, sound devices, Bluetooth, keyboard layouts and Wi-Fi: each item on the bar drops down its own panel.

Overview

Every workspace, live

Super+Down lays out your workspaces as large live cards. Jump to one, or rearrange them.

Notifications

Quiet until they matter

Pop-ups slide in where you want them, with a history in the bar to catch up on and Do not disturb for when you're busy.

Dock

Apps, windows and actions

A magnifying dock for pinned and running apps. Right-click an icon for its windows, its actions, or to pin it.

Lock and login

A calm way in

The lock screen and the greetd login screen match, with your wallpaper and picture. Lock, dim or suspend when you step away.

Install

Clone the repository and run the installer as your normal user. It installs what's needed for your distro, asks for your password for the system steps, and adds Slate to your login screen.

git clone https://github.com/daylerees/slate.git
cd slate
./install.sh

Then log out, pick Slate on the login screen and log in.

  •  Arch, Debian and Ubuntu, Fedora, openSUSE and NixOS
  •  Needs Hyprland 0.57 or newer and Quickshell 0.3 or newer
  •  Your current desktop and Hyprland config stay as they are
