What is Slate?

Slate is a desktop shell for Hyprland, the tiling Wayland compositor, built with Quickshell (QML). It adds everything you'd expect from a desktop around Hyprland's window management, in one consistent dark-slate design with the Geist font and Tabler icons.

It installs as its own Slate session, so your existing Hyprland setup is left alone: pick Slate on the login screen to use it, or your old desktop to go back. Everything is set from Settings, and anything you'd rather write by hand still goes in your own config files, which Slate never overwrites.

Slate is inspired by the best parts of macOS, Windows and Linux, and blends them together into something fresh. Things work the way you'd expect, so it's a welcoming place for people new to Linux and experienced users alike.

Everything a desktop needs



Menu bar

Workspaces and Spaces, the window title, app tray icons (tuck them away behind one button), notifications, clipboard, screenshots, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, keyboard layout, volume, battery and the clock. Every item drops down its own panel. Top, bottom or either side.



Magnifying dock

Pinned and running apps that grow under the pointer, bounce while they open, and have right-click menus for their windows and actions. Hides when a window needs the room.



Launcher

Tap Super and type. A list or a grid of apps, sorted by how often you use them; every setting is searchable from it too, and it does sums. Open apps on a new workspace with a key.



Switchers and overview

Super+Tab switches windows with live previews; Super+Down shows every workspace as a large live card to jump to or rearrange. Super alone opens the launcher, and every shortcut is listed and searchable in Settings.



Control centre, panels and notifications

Your profile, media, the weather and quick toggles, plus panels for networks, devices, sound, battery and the calendar. Notifications pop up where you want them, with a history and Do not disturb.



Theming that follows your wallpaper

Light, dark or automatic by sunset. Optionally, the accent and background tint can come from the wallpaper, which can change by itself. App icons come in tile, squircle, circle and more.



Displays

Arrange monitors by dragging, pick resolution, rotation and per-monitor scale, choose the main display, and size Slate itself. Changes revert by themselves unless you keep them.



Lock and login screens

A matching lock screen, and an optional greetd login screen, with your wallpaper and picture. Lock, turn the screen off or suspend when you're away, and lock before sleep.



A Settings app for everything

Appearance, displays, keyboard and mouse, sound, power, windows, shortcuts, default and startup apps, date and time, and more, with search across them all.



The little things

Night light on a schedule, hot corners, clipboard history, an emoji picker, screenshots, a right-click desktop menu, and password prompts that look the part.



Your apps, in Slate's colours

Switch on the apps you want to match: GTK and Qt apps, kitty, Alacritty, Ghostty, foot, WezTerm, VS Code, Zed, Helix, Vim, bat, fzf, Yazi, btop, Discord, Spotify, Firefox and more. They follow light, dark and your accent. Configs are backed up, and switching an app off puts it back.



Tiling or traditional windows

Windows tile themselves by default, or pick side stack, scrolling or stacking. Prefer the classic way? Switch to floating windows with title bars you can drag, plus close and maximise buttons (and double-click to maximise), in Settings → Windows.



Spaces

Arrange your apps across workspaces once, and save them as a space with a name, an icon and a colour. One click on the bar brings the whole set back on new workspaces, every window in its place. Restarting can bring all your apps back the same way.



Chalk, the assistant

Press Super+K and say what you want: "turn on glass", "put Firefox on workspace 2". An AI agent you already use does it through Slate's own tools, and Slate asks you before anything that can't be undone. Off until you switch it on, and yours to decide.

Theming

Colours that follow your wallpaper

Turn on colours from the wallpaper and Slate takes its accent and a gentle tint from whatever wallpaper you pick: the bar, panels, Settings and the apps you've switched on all shift together, light or dark. It's optional: keep your own accent if you prefer. The wallpaper can change by itself, on a timer or at login.

Tiling

Windows that arrange themselves

Open an app and Slate makes room for it: no dragging, no overlapping. Move focus, swap windows, flip a split or resize, all from the keyboard, and everything slides smoothly into place.

Floating windows

Or the way you're used to

Prefer windows you arrange yourself? Switch Settings → Windows to floating, and the window in front gets a title bar: drag it around, resize it from its corner, and maximise or close it with a click, much like on Windows. Slate draws the title bars itself, so there's nothing extra to install.

Launcher

Tap Super and type

Your apps, most used first, narrowing (and shrinking to fit) as you type. Every setting is searchable from here too, and it does sums: type one and the answer is ready to copy.

Control centre

Everything at a glance

Your profile, the time and today's weather, what's playing, and eight quick switches you choose: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Night Light, Do Not Disturb and more. One click on the slate in the corner of the bar.

Menu bar

Every item opens a panel

Power, the calendar and forecast, battery and power mode, sound devices, Bluetooth, keyboard layouts and Wi-Fi: each item on the bar drops down its own panel.

Settings

Everything in one place

One Settings app for the whole desktop: appearance and themes, windows, displays, the dock and bar, input, sound, power and more, with an overview of every page and a search across every setting.

Overview

Every workspace, live

Super+Down lays out your workspaces as large live cards. Jump to one, or hold Shift and rearrange them.

Spaces

Your workspaces, saved

Arrange your apps across workspaces once, then save them as a space with a name, an icon and a colour. One click brings the whole set back on new workspaces, every window in its place and marked with the space's colour.

Notifications

Quiet until they matter

Pop-ups slide in where you want them, with a history in the bar to catch up on and Do not disturb for when you're busy.

Dock

Apps, windows and actions

A magnifying dock for pinned and running apps. Right-click an icon for its windows, its actions, or to pin it.

Lock and login

A calm way in

The lock screen and the optional greetd login screen match, with your wallpaper and picture. Lock, dim or suspend when you step away.

Install

 Slate is an early preview. I use it every day, but it's young and moving quickly, so you may run into bugs. If you do, please tell me about them in a GitHub issue: it really helps, and thank you for trying it.

Clone the repository and run the installer as your normal user. It installs what's needed for your distro, asks for your password for the system steps, and adds Slate to your login screen.

git clone https://github.com/daylerees/slate.git
cd slate
./install.sh

Then log out, pick Slate on the login screen and log in.

  •  Arch, Debian and Ubuntu, Fedora, openSUSE and NixOS; and, new, without systemd on Artix, Void and Gentoo (with elogind)
  •  Needs Hyprland 0.57 or newer and Quickshell 0.3 or newer
  •  Your current desktop and Hyprland config stay as they are
