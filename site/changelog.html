 v0.28.0 12 November 2026

Features

  • Arrange the menu bar by dragging. Settings → Menu Bar → Edit layout (or right-click the bar) opens a panel under the bar. Drag things along the bar, into and out of containers, and to and from the pool of what isn't on it. Add containers and flexible spaces, split a container up, choose whether each thing shows its text, and put it all back with Defaults. Click anything for its options: the clock's format, date and seconds; whether a container collapses Never, When needed or Always, and its button's icon. Windows are dimmed and shortcuts stand down while you arrange.
  • A bar that copes when it doesn't fit. On a small screen, a bigger Interface size or with long names, the bar slims itself: the window's title shortens, text beside icons goes (longest first), the clock sheds its date and seconds, and "When needed" containers tuck behind their button. What still doesn't fit waits behind a new More button. Set priority chooses what stays longest, "Always keep on the bar" pins a thing, and "Try it shorter" shows what your bar would do.
  • A new default bar. Apps sits with Chalk, and Spaces with the window mode and the workspace dots. The window's icon and its title are separate things you can place. The Apps, Spaces, Chalk and window mode icons each take one pastel in the Colourful look.
  • Chalk, an assistant for your desktop (an experiment). Ask an AI agent you already use to change a setting, move a window or tell you what's where. It works through Slate's own tools (slate mcp), and Slate asks before anything that can't be undone. It's off until you switch it on in Settings → Assistant, which shows the risks first, and no agent is chosen until you choose one. Super+K opens it.
  • Glass you can tune. With glass on, the menu bar, its panels, the dock, notifications, the launcher and Chalk's window each have a Blur switch and their own opacity sliders. The bar's containers can fade, and firm up under the pointer. The bright edge round glass windows takes a tint of your accent.
  • One language for colour. What stays selected wears the accent; what the pointer is over gets a quieter highlight; anything destructive (Remove, Clear, Force Quit, Shut down) hovers red. The sheen is gone from buttons, switches, text boxes, dropdowns, selections and the bell panel's cards, so it's left for surfaces and app icons.
  • Rounded UI elements. Settings → Appearance has a switch that makes everything hard-edged and square: the bar's containers and panels, the dock, menus, buttons, switches and your windows. App icons and workspace dots keep the shapes chosen for them.
  • A gentle reload. When Slate restarts, the screen fades to "Reloading Slate" in your theme's colours and back, where it used to flash.
  • A Network page. The Wi-Fi page is now Network: how you're connected, each network socket and whether a cable's in, Wi-Fi and its networks, and the connection's details. On a cable the bar shows a wired icon.
  • Power. The power panel shows your battery's health and where charging stops, where the laptop reports them, and Settings → Power has a Charging limit switch. The processor, memory and disk bars turn yellow above 70% and red above 90%.
  • Slate fits the computer it's on. With no battery, the bar's battery item becomes a power button into the same panel. With no Wi-Fi or no speakers, that item leaves the bar and its panel says what's missing.
  • Region. The Date & Time page is now Region, with a date format (day, month or year first), the week's first day, and Celsius or Fahrenheit.
  • The clock's panel has the weather under the calendar: today as it is now, standing out, then the next three days.
  • Sound. The volume and microphone sliders show stripes drifting along them while they're in use, and red stripes while muted.
  • Workspaces. The indicator shown as you switch names the workspace. To start with, Super+Left/Right stop at the first and last workspace and there's always a spare empty one.
  • A hand pointer over everything in Slate you can click.
  • Settings. A back button on every page; fonts and default apps chosen from type-to-search dropdowns (fonts in their own typefaces); the All Settings search box stays put as the tiles scroll; Monochrome tray icons; shorter page names (Input, Printers, Security).
  • New defaults (your own settings are kept): title bar buttons on the left, focus follows the pointer, tiles in the icon's colour, a black screensaver, Gloss at 50% on a 0–100% slider.

Fixes

  • No desktop showing between workspaces as they slide, and no echo of the windows when sliding to an empty one. Windows go square at the corners while a switch slides.
  • A window opens in its own workspace's mode. One arriving from a tiled workspace while a floating one was in front used to float over the tiled windows, and the other way round.
  • Floating windows fit the screen. One that opens larger than nine tenths of the usable screen is made that size, and one hanging off an edge is moved in.
  • Clicks just under the menu bar get through. A hidden strip there swallowed them, so a floating window pushed up against the bar couldn't be grabbed by its title.
  • Picking a workspace from the overview sticks. With the zoom, Slate stopped watching for Hyprland handing focus back as soon as the overview had gone, so now and then the switch was undone.
  • Maximising twice in quick succession puts the window back, where it maximised twice over.
  • Notifications from apps that leave things out (no name, no icon, a picture that can't be loaded) show sensible stand-ins.
  • The dock's window previews are blurred behind with glass; the symbols on the dock's own buttons no longer wobble as they magnify.
  • The loading cursor no longer shifts left and right as it turns.
  • Interface size reaches the reload cover and the bar's arranging panel, and the hot corner check after unlocking works at any size.
  • Updating the login screen ends a "Later" from before, so the next change is asked about.
  • Settings: a slider's changed-from-default hint says the real default; the position pickers' edges are easier to point at; pointing at a search result no longer looks like choosing it; "None found" where a missing sound device showed -1.

Other

  • Removed: the bar's hover descriptions and their setting; the Shine setting (the rim is always on with glass); the "show on the menu bar" switches on the Workspaces, Spaces and Assistant pages, and the left-side, right-side and clock rows on the Menu Bar page. All of that is done in Edit layout now.
  • Wi-Fi sits next to the battery on the bar; the clock's text changes without a fade.
  • Testing is now one test and fix cycle before each release.
  • The whole test suite passed (3,943 tests) one commit before this release; the last change, to the workspace overview, was checked by running its tests repeatedly.
  • Tested with Hyprland 0.57.1 and Quickshell 0.3.2 (the pinned test image); built and used day to day on Hyprland 0.57.2. The tests weren't run again with today's Arch packages for this release, as neither Hyprland nor Quickshell has had a release since they last were (10 November, one small difference in the emoji picker's screenshot).

 v0.27.0 10 November 2026

Features

  • Window modes. Besides tiling and floating, windows can now be arranged as a Side Stack (one main window, the rest beside it), Scrolling (columns on a strip that scrolls sideways), Stacking (one window at a time, the others behind it) or Solo (every window on a workspace of its own). Settings → Windows sets the default, and Super+J and the focus keys follow the mode you're in.
  • A mode for each workspace. A new button beside the workspace dots shows the mode of the workspace you're on; its panel gives that workspace a mode of its own. Changing the default with windows open asks whether to tidy every workspace into the new mode or leave them as they are. Spaces remember each workspace's mode.
  • Name your workspaces. The pencil on that panel names a workspace ("Coding", "Music"). The name takes the place of "Workspace 3" in the workspace switcher, the bar and the Spaces panel, stays while the workspace is around, and is saved with a Space.
  • Slate Bars: Slate's own title bars, now the default. Floating windows get a title bar Slate draws itself, on the window in front: nothing to build, and nothing that breaks when Hyprland updates. Hyprbars (the plugin, with a bar on every floating window) is now an option you choose in Settings → Windows, which explains what's involved and builds it for you. If you already had Hyprbars working, you keep it.
  • Hyprbars looks after itself across Hyprland updates. A build made for another Hyprland is never loaded, so there are no more red errors at login. Slate switches to Slate Bars, asks whether to rebuild, offers a reload when it's done, then asks whether to switch back. If Hyprland was updated but not yet restarted, it offers to rebuild ahead of time.
  • Ten wallpapers of Slate's own, in 4K: soft glows of colour over a deep base, each with Slate's mark in the middle or plain. Ember, Iris, Lagoon and Blossom in mixed colours; Fern, Cobalt, Ruby, Honey, Graphite and Mist in single ones. They're listed below your own pictures in Settings → Appearance (a switch hides them), and are drawn without colour banding.
  • More ways to switch. The window switcher and the workspace switcher each have a choice of animations (slide, hop, wheel, tilt, orbit, deck, fan or instant), and the workspace switcher can be a grid. Holding Super, the arrow keys move through the window switcher.
  • Dock hover effects. Magnify, tilt, fade, glow or raise (Settings → Dock). Window previews fade in from their icon, scroll sideways when there are many, and can appear instantly: a switch turns them on or off, and a slider sets the wait (now 0.2 s by default).
  • The login screen is opt-in. Installing Slate no longer touches your login screen. Settings → Lock & Idle has Install (with a plain account of the risks), Update and Switch back.
  • App Theming knows more about each app. A description, its website, an Install or Open button, and whether it's installed. Flatpak builds of Discord, VS Code, Zed, Helix, Neovim, zathura, WezTerm, Text Editor and Firefox are themed too.
  • Themes that survive app updates. When a Spotify or Discord update removes Slate's theme (Spicetify's and Vencord's patches), Slate notices within a few minutes, puts it back and tells you to restart the app.
  • Without systemd: Artix, Void and Gentoo. Slate now runs where elogind stands in for systemd. This is new and hasn't been tried on real installs yet: docs/INSTALL.md says what's needed and what's different (Slate shows you the command to switch a service on rather than doing it).
  • A warmer voice. The Welcome window, questions, notifications and empty screens are friendlier, and the Welcome window follows your theme (glass included), lets you choose Glass or flat, and explains what the Super key is.
  • Smaller things: a note in the middle of the screen when the secret workspace is empty; a themed Slate logo as the session fades in; removing a startup app asks first; every question in Settings uses Slate's main confirmation window.

Fixes

  • Tray icons no longer go missing for apps such as Mullvad after Slate reloads or at login.
  • No more flash of the default colours when Slate reloads: your settings and the wallpaper's colours are in place before anything is drawn.
  • Pressing Print Screen repeatedly opened a new area chooser each time, dimming the screen further with every press. Only one opens now.
  • A config error at login when Hyprbars wasn't loaded is gone.
  • Switching windows now and then did nothing when the computer was busy: the switcher asked Hyprland to focus the window a moment too early. It checks and asks again.
  • Windows opened on a floating workspace stayed floating when that workspace went back to tiling. They're tiled again now.
  • The menu bar's icons are all drawn the same height and lined up; the window mode button sits closer to its neighbours.
  • Hyprbars' top edge resizes the window.
  • The diagonal resize cursors point the right way.

Other

  • "Master & stack" is now called Side Stack, and "Monocle" is Stacking.
  • The installer no longer builds Hyprbars. If you choose it, building needs a compiler, git, Hyprland's headers and a connection.
  • Slate's wallpapers add about 46 MB to a download of Slate.
  • Slate is an early preview: you may run into bugs. Please report them in GitHub issues.
  • Updating may ask twice for your password: once for the login session's start script, and (if you use Slate's login screen) once for the login screen.
  • Heads-up: with today's Arch packages, one test screenshot (an emoji in the emoji picker) still draws slightly differently, as in v0.26.0. That check ran earlier today, before this release's changes went in.

 v0.26.0 10 November 2026

Features

  • Settings → Webcam. Your camera's picture settings: brightness, contrast, colour, sharpness, gain, white balance and exposure (automatic or manual), anti-flicker for your mains (50 or 60 Hz) and backlight compensation. Webcams forget these when they're unplugged or restarted, so Slate puts yours back at login. A preview turns the camera on only when you ask, and the page shows which apps are using the camera.
  • Drag apps on the dock. The icon comes away with the pointer at its magnified size, and the others make room around a placeholder that shows where it'll land. Drop a running app among your pinned ones to pin it, or pull a pinned app well up off the dock to unpin it (a warning appears first; it stays on the dock while it's running). Window previews and menus stay out of the way while you drag.
  • See which settings you've changed. A small dot after a setting's name means it isn't at its default; point at the dot to see what the default is.
  • Move Settings by its frame. Drag the Slate mark at the top of the sidebar, or the empty space in the page header.

Fixes

  • Moving the dock or bar to another edge slides it out and back in at the new edge, instead of zooming across the screen and flickering where it was. It shows there for a moment even with auto-hide, so you see where it went.
  • Menu points on glass. The little point on dock and tray menus showed as a whole diamond with glass on.
  • Window previews sit above magnified icons, rather than behind them.
  • Settings comes back on the page it was on after Slate reloads, instead of All Settings.
  • In dark mode without glass, the Apps, media, Downloads and Waste Basket tiles on the dock are a shade lighter, so they stand out from the dock.

Other

  • Settings → Webcam uses v4l2-ctl (the v4l-utils package). Install it to use the page (or run ./install.sh); without it, the page says what's missing.
  • Heads-up: with today's Arch packages, one test screenshot (an emoji in the emoji picker) draws slightly differently, likely a newer emoji font. Nothing else changed.

 v0.25.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • HDR in Settings → Displays, for displays that support it: Auto (HDR while a game or video fills the screen with HDR content), On (always, in 10-bit colour, with how bright and colourful everything else looks) or Off. Like the rest of the page, a change reverts by itself unless you keep it.
  • Solid, glossy icon tiles with glass. The tile behind an app icon was a faint film over whatever was behind it; it's solid now, with the frosting and sheen on top, everywhere icons show: the dock, launcher, switchers, notifications, the bar and Spaces. The dock's sheen no longer fades as an icon magnifies, and magnified tiles keep the dock's glass behind them.
  • Dock badges stay until you go to the app. For apps that don't publish their own count (mail and chat apps such as Thunderbird), the badge counts their notifications since you last went to the app, rather than clearing when you open the notification panel.
  • Reload everything in Settings → Debug: reloads Hyprland's config and restarts Slate from scratch, for when something looks stuck. Your apps stay open.

Fixes

  • Tray icons stay when Slate restarts. Apps that only sign up their tray icon once (Mullvad, Spotify, Discord and other Electron apps) lost it whenever Slate restarted, including after updates. Reopen such an app once after updating, and its icon then stays.
  • Glass works without wallpaper tint. With glass on, the dark theme and Tint from wallpaper off, the dock, notifications and other glass surfaces were fully see-through.
  • App badges (unread counts, progress) are kept when Slate restarts, instead of disappearing until each app sends a new one.
  • Slate no longer leaves a background process running each time it reloads.
  • Settings → Displays opens on a display that's in use, not one that's turned off.

Other

  • Slate now needs Python's GObject bindings (python-gobject, python3-gi…) for the tray fix; it offers to install them after updating (or run ./install.sh). Without them the tray works as before.
  • The tests now run in full on GitHub before each release, screenshots included.

 v0.24.0 9 November 2026

Fixes

  • The login screen no longer always looks out of date. On systems without diffutils, Slate's checks for an outdated login screen, missing packages and changed files always failed. It's now one of Slate's packages; Slate offers to install it after updating (or run ./install.sh).
  • A grey wallpaper no longer gives a pink accent colour: it keeps the default, as a black one does.
  • With whole app icons, an app with no icon no longer shows a dark square in light mode.
  • Switching an app's theming off (Settings → App Theming) puts its config back exactly: a file without a final newline stays that way, and KDE's settings no longer gain a blank line.
  • Settings → Updates now warns about a file of yours like settings.json.bak in Slate's folder, which an update would remove.
  • The clipboard history recovers from a damaged history file, and copying a picture whose file has gone fails quietly instead of with an error.
  • Downloading screensaver scenery no longer stops on a video size it doesn't know; it fetches that video again.
  • Printers found to add (Settings → Printers & Scanners) show the name they give themselves rather than their address when their model is unknown.
  • Creating your own config files never writes through a link of yours, even one that points nowhere.
  • Fewer warnings in Slate's log from the launcher, the clipboard and emoji picker, and the login screen.

Other

  • Slate has a test suite. Over 1,300 checks (every setting and Settings page, panels, the dock, the login screen, scripts, and screenshots of each part) now run on every change and before every release, and weekly against the newest Arch packages, to catch breakage before it reaches you.

 v0.23.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • Updates install what they need. When a new version needs packages you don't have, updating from Settings asks for your password and installs them, instead of sending you to the installer. A changed login session is updated the same way.
  • Slate checks for missing pieces. Shortly after it starts, if a package it needs is missing or the login session is out of date, a notification offers to install it (Later waits a week). Optional extras are still offered by ./install.sh; NixOS keeps managing packages in its own configuration.

Fixes

  • Updating on Fedora no longer stops on the extra repositories in Slate's package list, which it mistook for missing packages.

 v0.22.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • The launcher finds much more. Search forgives typos and initials, and finds open windows, apps' own actions, recent files and folders, unit and date conversions (5 km in miles, today + 30 days), > command to run something, :name for emoji, and a web search as the last result. Each can be switched off in Settings → Launcher → What it finds.
  • Quick toggles in the launcher: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Do Not Disturb, night light and game mode show their state with a switch, flipped from the results.
  • Recent apps first before you type anything.
  • Wrap-around: the arrow keys go from the last result back to the first (on by default).

Fixes

  • Icons in the launcher's results no longer flicker as you type, and the arrow keys no longer scroll the results out of view.
  • Commands and file names keep their own capitalisation.
  • Works better beyond Arch: apps, icons, layouts and sessions are found wherever the system keeps them (NixOS, Flatpak, /usr/local); default apps are whichever you have installed; the login screen installer finds greetd's user; the GTK theme is set only when installed; setting your picture explains what's missing.

Other

  • The installer sets up everything Slate needs on a fresh system: the colour emoji font, AccountsService and optional extras, a terminal and file manager if you have none, and Bluetooth and NetworkManager. Run ./install.sh once after updating.

 v0.21.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • A media player in the dock. While something's playing, right after the Apps button: its album art with Previous, Play/Pause and Next beside it and a progress bar beneath. Click the art to go to the app that's playing; right-click for the controls. The album art can be switched off.
  • A Waste Basket at the end of the dock, filled when there's something in it. Click to open it; right-click to empty it, which asks first. Drop files on it to bin them.
  • A Downloads stack before the Waste Basket: click for a grid of your newest downloads, pictures as thumbnails; right-click to open the folder.
  • Window previews. Pause on an app with windows open to see them live, and click one to go to it. Settings → Dock has a slider for how long to pause (or Off).
  • Badges and progress. An app's unread count on its icon (what the app says itself, or its unread notifications), and a progress bar while it downloads or copies.
  • Drag and drop in the dock. Drag pinned apps to rearrange them, or up off the dock to unpin. Drop files on an app to open them in it.
  • Dividers after the media player, between pinned apps and the others, and before Downloads.
  • Each of these has a switch in Settings → Dock, and they follow your icon shape, tinting, glass, and light or dark.

Fixes

  • Live window previews aren't dimmed any more. Windows without focus came out greyed in the switcher, the overview, the Spaces panel and the dock's previews.
  • Smaller screens. The notification and Wi-Fi panels, the dock, the clipboard and emoji picker, tray menus, the welcome and the avatar picker now fit small or highly scaled screens.
  • Spotify theming works when Spicetify was installed with its own installer.

Other

  • Every card in Settings has a label above it.

 v0.20.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • Printers & Scanners. A new page in All Settings. It shows your printers with what they're doing and the jobs waiting; make one the default, print a test page, cancel jobs or remove one. Find printers finds printers on your network or plugged in by USB that need no driver (most from the last ten years) and adds them in one click; the first one you add becomes your default. Any other printer goes through the printer settings app, or CUPS's own page. Scanners are listed too, with a button to scan in your scanning app. If printing or scanning isn't set up, the page says what to install, and turning the printing service on is a button. Printing and scanning are optional packages for every distro Slate supports.

Fixes

  • Opening Settings brings it to you. With Settings already open on another workspace, opening it again (from the bar, the launcher, a shortcut or a link) now moves it to the workspace you're on, instead of leaving it out of sight.

Other

  • New optional packages: CUPS, the printer settings app, SANE with network scanner support, and Document Scanner. Updating from Settings will ask you to run ./install.sh once to install them.

 v0.19.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • Eight more scenery videos. Get scenery videos now fetches twelve clips: Lake Bled at sunrise and from the air, Right Whale Bay and Gold Harbour on South Georgia, an emperor penguin in Antarctica, the Crimean coast, the Blue Ridge mountains and a beach in Cádiz join the four from before. In all: about 410 MB at 1080p, 780 MB at 1440p, 1.1 GB at 4K.
  • Glassier notifications. With glass on, notifications get a curved glossy shine across their top, and their app icons a sheen like the dock's.
  • A steadier menu bar with glass. In dark mode the bar is a little darker and less see-through than other glass, so it reads as a steady band whatever the wallpaper does.
  • The dock stays open over floating windows. With auto-hide on, a workspace with only floating windows keeps the dock open, as an empty one does (except in floating windows mode).
  • Long window titles fade out before the bar's status icons, instead of running underneath them.

Fixes

  • Turning tap to click off in Settings now works (Hyprland was refusing it).
  • Apps keep the cursor size set in Settings, rather than getting the old one back after a theme change.
  • The login screen no longer runs your startup apps or holds on to the lid switch.
  • The calendar and control centre clocks follow the 24-hour setting.
  • In game mode the bar's separators go solid with the rest of the bar.
  • The switcher and overview slide at the Animations speed (Fast and Off).
  • Long Wi-Fi names in Settings stop short of the buttons.
  • A broken wallpaper can't hang the accent colour pick.
  • slate ipc call bar panel gamemode opens the game mode panel.
  • The package lists now include numpy and rsvg-convert, which app icons need. Updating from Settings will ask you to run ./install.sh once to install them.

Other

  • A lighter Slate overall. Many things that ran all the time now only run when needed: the lock screen's Caps Lock check, the hidden clipboard list, Settings' clock and pages while it's closed, the bar's timers on every screen, and more. The window switcher captures each window once instead of several times, the dock no longer redraws its icon tiles on every frame of magnification, and Slate's scripts start far fewer processes. Everything looks and works as before.
  • The code is tidier underneath: shared pieces in place of repeated ones, clearer names, and comments that match what the code does.

 v0.18.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • Scenery videos in up to 4K. Get scenery videos now downloads each clip in the best size it comes in for your screen: 4K on screens wider than 2560 pixels, 1440p on mid-size ones, otherwise 1080p. A new Scenery quality setting, just under the button in Settings → Screensaver, lets you choose 4K, 1440p or 1080p yourself. Getting them again later only replaces the clips that get sharper. 4K plays smoothly and lightly with hardware video decoding.

Fixes

  • Far fewer "Login screen out of date" prompts. It now only asks when the login screen itself has changed, not with nearly every Slate update. Later now waits a week. Settings → Lock & Idle still offers Update whenever you like.
  • Previewing the screensaver no longer closes Settings, so you're back where you were when it ends.

 v0.17.0 9 November 2026

Features

  • Game mode. A new button for the bar's status icons (Settings → Menu Bar → Game mode, off by default), showing On or Off. Its panel turns off everything costly or distracting while you play: glass, blur and shadows; see-through windows (terminals included); animations; live window previews (the switcher and overview show each window's outline and app icon instead); and notification pop-ups (they still go to the list). The touchpad also keeps working while you type. Turning it off puts everything back exactly as you had it.
  • Screensaver videos. A new Videos style plays the videos in a folder (~/Videos/Screensaver), silent and fading from one to the next. Get scenery videos downloads four free clips: Earth from the space station, an aurora over Lapland, clouds over a canyon and a waterfall in Tasmania (about 95 MB, with a progress bar and credits). Needs Qt Multimedia.
  • The lock screen crossfades in over your desktop and back out after your password, while staying fully locked the whole time. Locks before sleep don't fade.
  • Search every setting from any Settings page, with a search box at the top of each page.
  • Settings comes back where it was (same page, same scroll position) when Slate reloads or restarts.
  • Tiles in the icon's colour: the tile behind each app icon can take a deep shade of the icon's own colour, with a soft gradient (Settings → Appearance → App icons).
  • Window borders get a sheen with glass on, catching the light at the top left corner.
  • A Slate Default theme preset, and presets now set folder colours from the wallpaper too. Restore defaults on Appearance goes back to Slate Default.
  • Light mode asks first, as it's still being polished.
  • A picker window for your account picture, with your picture folders, a thumbnail grid and a round preview.
  • Your location searches as you type, and shows the place you've set.
  • The time zone is a dropdown you can search.
  • Refresh rate has its own dropdown, next to resolution.
  • A play button for each sound in Settings → Sound.
  • The workspace overview and window switcher can show live previews, app icons or both.
  • Animations Off (and Faster) now apply to Slate's own animations (panels, the bar, fades), not just windows and workspaces.
  • The Settings window never dims when it isn't focused, so you can watch a change take effect.
  • Dropdowns show when there's more to scroll, with soft shadows and a slim scrollbar.
  • Urgent login screen notices: the prompt to update the login screen, and the notice if updating fails, now stay until you've seen them.
  • More: a switch for game mode's On/Off text, a Spaces button switch on the Menu Bar page, App icons previews that show every icon setting and glass, Slate's mark on its notifications coloured like the bar's button, and a copyright line on the About page.

Fixes

  • After you unlocked, Slate still thought the screen was locked, so hot corners, the screensaver and the idle lock stopped working until Slate reloaded. Fixed.
  • A hot corner used to lock the screen works again after you unlock, and screens turned off from a corner come back on at your next mouse move or key press.

 v0.1.0 8 November 2026

The first release of Slate: a calm, complete desktop for Linux, built on Hyprland and Quickshell. It installs as its own Slate session, so your existing setup is left alone.

What's in it

  • Menu bar with workspaces, the window title, app tray, notifications, Wi-Fi, keyboard layout, Bluetooth, volume, battery and the clock; every item opens its own panel.
  • Magnifying dock with pinned and running apps, right-click menus and auto-hide.
  • Launcher (tap Super) for apps, every setting and quick sums.
  • Window switcher (Super+Tab) and workspace overview (Super+Down) with live previews.
  • Tiling by default, or optional floating windows with title bars (hyprbars).
  • Control centre with your profile, media, weather and quick toggles.
  • Notifications with a history and Do not disturb; on-screen indicators.
  • Theming: dark, light or automatic, an accent and tint that can follow the wallpaper, icon shapes, and opt-in theming for other apps (GTK, Qt, terminals, editors and more).
  • Lock and login screens that match, with idle lock, screen-off and suspend.
  • Settings app covering all of it, with search.
  • Clipboard history, emoji picker, colour picker, screenshots, night light and hot corners.
